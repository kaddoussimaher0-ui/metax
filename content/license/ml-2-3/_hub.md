# The license, read as a single instrument in nine parts

This group holds the Maher License 2.3 itself, part by part, as a single readable instrument rather than a register of fragments. It exists because a license that is only summarised is a license that cannot be checked, and the estate's whole claim is that its governing instrument is legible to the reader it binds. Every part below is a route with its own detailed page, and a plain-text mirror carries the entire license in a form that survives having its stylesheet disabled.

## How the license is structured

ML-2.3 runs Part 0 through Part H, then five schedules. Part 0 states the preamble and the standing of the instrument. Part A defines its terms operatively, so that a dispute turns on a written definition rather than an intuition. Part B is the grant — what a licensee actually receives. Part C states the ethical conditions on use, including the four grounds of deception, harm, oppression, and betrayal of trust, and the bounded absolute prohibition of core Riba. Part D covers notices and disclosure. Part E is governance. Part F is breach and termination, where the condition-versus-covenant distinction lives. Part G is interpretation. Part H is the structural-harm register — the contribution that distinguishes ML-2.3 from an ordinary conduct license.

## The map — what lives in this group

Each part is a leaf page. **Part 0 · Preamble & Standing** states why the instrument exists and what authority it claims. **Part A · Definitions** fixes the vocabulary. **Part B · Grant** states the permission given. **Part C · Ethical Conditions** carries the four grounds and Riba. **Part D · Notices & Disclosure** governs how a licensee must attribute and disclose. **Part E · Governance** states how the license is stewarded. **Part F · Breach & Termination** states what breach does. **Part G · Interpretation** supplies the reading rules. **Part H · Structural Harms** incorporates the twenty-five-harm register. **Schedules 1–5** carry the forms, including the Part H Statement form. And the **Plain-Text Mirror** is the whole license in one legible file.

## The worked frame — why the plain-text mirror exists

A license that can only be read through a stylesheet is a license that fails its own accessibility standard, and one whose meaning depends on JavaScript is worse. The plain-text mirror is the estate's answer: the entire instrument, in one file, legible with CSS disabled and with JavaScript off. It is the version a court, an auditor, or a screen reader would read, and its existence is a direct application of the estate's static principle — that the load-bearing text must survive the failure of everything decorative around it.

## Limits of this group

These pages present the license; they do not adjudicate it. The estate's curator is explicitly not a lawyer, and the instrument itself flags that the condition-versus-covenant line and the enforceability of use restrictions need review by counsel before ML-2.3 issues, with that review status printed rather than assumed. The Islamic-ethics content is a reasoned analogy by a non-scholar, filed as an open question. These pages state the text and its intent; they do not claim it has been ruled valid.

## The falsifier for this group

The governing claim is that a license read as a single legible instrument is complied with better than one known only through summaries. The refutation: if licensees who read the full parts comply no better than those who read only the summary, the parts add length without effect and the summary should govern.

## What this group asks of you

Read Part A before you argue about any other part, because the definitions decide most disputes before they start. Read Part H if you deploy at any scale, because its seven absolutes bind you irrespective of size. And if you cite the license, cite the plain-text mirror, not a rendered page — under ML-2.3's own notices, the mirror is the authoritative English text.
