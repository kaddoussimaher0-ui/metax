# The clause that makes structural harm operative

Part H is the contribution that distinguishes ML-2.3 from an ordinary conduct license. Where Part C conditions use on four grounds written for conduct — deception, harm, oppression, and betrayal of trust — Part H reaches a class those grounds cannot: structural harm, the characteristic injury of computing, which no actor intends, which arises from how a system is built and financed, which is distributed so thinly across so many people that no single instance is actionable, and which is invisible to the party causing it because the metric by which that party judges itself is the very instrument of the harm. Part H closes that hole in the middle of Part C. This page is the clause itself; the register it points at lives one route over.

# What Part H incorporates and defines

Part H is short, because its content lives in the register it incorporates by reference. The clause does four things a register cannot do for itself. **§H.2** names the twenty-five harms and incorporates HX-000, the register, with a rule that an expired verify tag downgrades the affected evidence to contested and suspends a harm whose entire evidence base is contested. **§H.1-bis** defines the four roles — Author, Integrator, Operator, Commissioner — so every duty can state which party it binds. **§H.2-bis** sets the proportionality scale gate, three tiers by persons affected, so the smallest deployers owe the least. And **§H.3** states the seven absolutes and, at §H.3(b), what breach does.

# How the clause is structured

The clause register runs from §H.1, scope and non-exhaustiveness, through §H.30, the language-parity duty. The absolutes sit at §H.3(a)(i)–(vii): HX-01 attention expropriation, HX-04 the human as product, HX-06 algorithmic governance without due process, HX-03 synthetic provenance, HX-14 moral deskilling in part, HX-24 developmental capture, and HX-15 false compliance representation. Everything else is a duty — a covenant, not a condition. The register carries twenty-seven duties in total, most binding the Operator, some the Commissioner, and several binding the Steward of the license itself. The seven-absolutes-of-twenty-five ratio, twenty-eight percent, is defended in the drafting: a register in which everything is absolute is one in which nothing is.

# The worked instance — breach, at §H.3(b)

The clause's most consequential provision is what breach does, because without it Part H would be an essay. §H.3(b) draws a line: the seven absolutes operate as **conditions on the scope of the grant** — use outside them is unlicensed, and breach is an infringement that terminates automatically, subject to the ninety-day cure window where cure is possible, which it is not for a completed absolute breach. Every other Part H duty operates as a **covenant** — breach sounds in contract, does not terminate the grant automatically, and is remedied by notice, cure within ninety days, and registry annotation if uncured. And the clause states expressly that Part H creates **no third-party right of action**: an affected person is not a party, and pretending otherwise would be the inverse solutionism the register's method warns against.

# Limits of this clause

The condition-versus-covenant distinction is jurisdiction-sensitive — drawn differently across legal systems, and the enforceability of use restrictions in copyright licenses is contested in several. Part H is drafted by a curator who is explicitly not a lawyer, and it flags that it needs review by counsel in the primary governing jurisdiction and by a qualified scholar before ML-2.3 issues, with that review status printed on the license page rather than assumed. The clause governs; where it and the register differ, the clause governs and the difference is a filed defect.

# The falsifier for this clause

Part H's governing claim is that incorporating an operative, falsifiable register — with roles, a scale gate, and a stated breach consequence — changes conduct more than a general good-faith clause does. The refutation: if published Part H Statements predict no measurable reduction in the harms they name, at effect sizes distinguishable from selection, then Part H is admired rather than obeyed, which is the very conduct HX-15 describes, committed by the instrument itself.

# What this clause obliges

Determine your role under §H.1-bis and your tier under §H.2-bis before you read any duty; both decide what you owe. Read the seven absolutes at §H.3(a) regardless of tier, because they never scale down. And resolve the whole clause into the one artefact it turns on — the Part H Statement — which the Compliance group shows you how to write, and which the estate has written for itself, marking HX-01 and HX-04 unremedied.
