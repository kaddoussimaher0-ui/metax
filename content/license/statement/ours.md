# The estate holding itself to the register it publishes

This is MetaX.Academy's own Part H Statement, filed under ML-2.3 §H.2-bis(d). It exists because a register of harms published by an estate that would not apply the register to itself would be the exact conduct the register names in HX-15 — adopting an ethics instrument as a shield. The statement is deliberately uncomfortable in two places, and those two places are the point of publishing it at all. Everything below renders from the estate's own dated assessment, not from marketing copy, and it is meant to be read against the advertising policy and the estate-status pages it links.

# Declared role and scale tier

MetaX.Academy declares the role of **Operator** — it runs a deployment against real persons, the learners and readers who use the estate — and, for the parts of the estate it authors, **Author**. Its declared scale tier under §H.2-bis is the **lowest tier, below ten thousand distinct persons affected in any twelve-month period,** because the live site is a demo with limited traffic. At that tier a deployer owes the seven absolutes and this one-page statement, and nothing more. The estate nonetheless completes the fuller disclosure below, because it authors the register and cannot credibly ask larger deployers to disclose what it will not.

# Harms judged applicable and unremedied

Two harms are marked applicable and **unremedied**, both dated 2026-09-01, both for the same reason.

**HX-01 — Attention Expropriation (§H.3(a)(i), absolute).** The estate's content plane carries AdSense and Adsterra advertising slots. An advertising-funded surface derives value from engagement, which engages the variable-ratio, duration-sensitive mechanism HX-01 names. The estate does not run behavioural profiling of learners and does not optimise session duration, which narrows the exposure — but the presence of ad slots on the content plane is enough to keep the disposition honest at **unremedied**. Remedy available: remove the ad slots. Because the site is a demo, this window is open and cheap now, and taking it would let the disposition read *remedied*.

**HX-04 — The Human as Product (§H.3(a)(ii), absolute).** Advertising slots imply an advertising relationship in which the reader's attention is the good being sold, which is the transaction HX-04 describes. The estate carries no third-party analytics and sells no data, which again narrows the exposure — but an ad-funded surface is, by construction, one in which the reader is partly the product, so the disposition is **unremedied** for as long as the slots remain. Remedy available: the same removal that remedies HX-01 remedies this.

The estate records these two marks rather than omitting them, because under the register's own adverse-findings test, a party that has never published a finding against itself is doing compliance theatre. These are the estate's findings against itself.

# Harms judged applicable and remedied

Several harms are applicable to an educational estate and are handled by structure rather than promise. **HX-03 (synthetic provenance)** is remedied by MX-007, which discloses machine drafting on every page. **HX-15 (ethics laundering)** is remedied by the public defect register and the requirement that the estate publish adverse findings — this statement is that requirement in force. **HX-19 (interpretive concentration)** is mitigated, not fully remedied: the estate has one curator, and the residual exposure is disclosed rather than resolved, with MX-016 appeals and the public determination queue as partial structure. **HX-24 (developmental capture)** is remedied structurally by the estate-wide absence of behavioural advertising and profiling, which makes profiling of any learner impossible rather than merely prohibited.

# Harms judged inapplicable, with the safe harbour relied on

Many harms do not attach to a static educational estate. **HX-21 (the unconsented experiment)** is inapplicable: the estate runs no behavioural experimentation on learners, and MX-009's prohibition of behavioural advertising makes this structural. **HX-23 (synthetic relation)** is inapplicable: the estate has no companion surface and AG-000's sole-prompter rule means no agent maintains a relationship with any learner. **HX-26-class experimentation** duties do not attach for the same reason. Each inapplicability rests on a stated safe-harbour limb, not on a bare assertion.

# The unnamed-harm review (§H.21)

Reviewing the estate's own deployment for structural harms the register does not reach, one candidate is recorded: the social-only sign-in providers serve the Arabic-market audience worse than the English one, which is both an HX-13 (exclusion by default) and an HX-25 (linguistic flattening) concern and is filed as a defect against the estate rather than left unnamed. No other structural harm of the estate's own deployment was identified beyond those named above.

# Limits of this statement

This is a self-assessment by the curator, not an audit by an independent party, and it should be read as such. The scale-tier declaration rests on the estate's own traffic estimate. The Islamic-ethics groundings the marks rely on are reasoned extensions by a non-scholar, filed as open questions. And the statement is dated: it is true as of its review date and no later, and it must be re-filed when the ad slots change or the estate leaves demo.

# The falsifier for this statement

The statement's governing claim is that naming HX-01 and HX-04 unremedied, in public, two clicks from the license, is more honest than a remediated claim the estate cannot support. The refutation: if the ad slots are removed and this statement is not promptly updated to read *remedied*, the statement is stale rather than honest, and the failure is the estate's own — file it as a defect.

# What this statement obliges the estate to do

Either remove the advertising slots and re-file this statement with HX-01 and HX-04 marked remedied, or keep the honest text in place for exactly as long as the slots remain. There is no third option in which the estate keeps the ad revenue and claims the harms remedied. That is the discipline the register imposes on everyone who adopts it, and the estate takes it first.
