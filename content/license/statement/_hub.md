# The single compliance surface the whole license resolves to

This group is where twenty-five harms and twenty-seven duties become usable. Their absence from a single surface would guarantee the very harm the register names as HX-15 — compliance theatre — because a well-meaning licensee who cannot tell what they owe will adopt the instrument as reputation rather than as change. Part H therefore prescribes exactly one artefact: the Part H Statement, one page, published at a stated URL. This group holds the form, a tool to generate it, the estate's own filed statement, worked examples at each scale tier, and the compatibility notes for combining ML-2.3 with SPDX-listed licenses.

## What the statement contains

A Part H Statement carries seven things and nothing else: the declared role — Author, Integrator, Operator, or Commissioner; the declared scale tier by persons affected; the harms judged applicable, one line each; the harms judged inapplicable, each with the safe harbour relied on; any harm judged applicable and unremedied, with a date; the unnamed-harm review naming any structural harm of the deployment the register does not reach; and a date and a version. A licensee below ten thousand persons owes this statement, the seven absolutes, and nothing more. This single page is the most important addition in ML-2.3, because it is the difference between an instrument that is complied with and one that is merely admired.

## The map — what lives in this group

Each leaf is a compliance tool. **Statement Form (Schedule 1)** is the canonical template with every field explained. **Statement Generator** is a client-side tool that assembles a statement from a licensee's answers, producing text they publish themselves — the estate stores nothing. **MetaX's Own Statement** is the estate's filed Part H Statement, which currently marks HX-01 and HX-04 unremedied because of the advertising slots on the content plane. **Worked Examples by Tier** shows a completed statement for a small deployment, a mid-scale one, and a large one. **Compatibility & SPDX** states how ML-2.3 combines with other licenses and what an SPDX identifier for it would carry.

## The worked frame — the adverse-findings test

The register's cheapest and hardest-to-fake honesty test is whether an adopter has ever published a finding against itself. The Part H Statement operationalises it: a statement that names a harm as applicable and unremedied, with a date, is a published adverse finding. A sincere adopter has such findings and is willing to publish them, at no cost; an insincere one is forced either to publish or to claim a completeness no reader will believe. MetaX's own statement is the estate eating this rule first — it names two unremedied harms on its own front-door instrument rather than hiding them.

## Limits of this group

The generator is a convenience, not an authority; a statement it produces is only as honest as the answers given, and the estate does not review or store it. The worked examples are illustrative, not safe harbours — copying one does not discharge a duty a different deployment carries. And SPDX compatibility is stated as the estate's reading, pending the counsel review the license flags, rather than as a registered fact.

## The falsifier for this group

The governing claim is that a single one-page statement makes Part H compliable where the full register alone would not. The refutation: if licensees given the statement form comply no more completely than those given only the register, the statement adds a surface without adding compliance and the register should stand alone.

## What this group asks of you

Write your statement early and publish it at a stable URL, because a statement that cannot be found is not published. Name your unremedied harms with dates rather than omitting them — the omission is the harm. And read the estate's own statement before you write yours: it is the worked example that matters most, because it shows the estate holding itself to the test it asks of you.
