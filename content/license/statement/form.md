# The one page the whole license turns on

Twenty-five harms and twenty-seven duties are unusable without a single compliance surface, and their absence would guarantee the conduct HX-15 names — well-meaning licensees adopting the instrument as reputation because they cannot tell what they owe. Schedule 1 prescribes exactly one artefact against that failure: the Part H Statement, one page, published at a stated URL. This page is the canonical template, field by field. It is the difference between an instrument that is complied with and one that is merely admired, and it is what every chartered academy must publish under requirement eleven.

# What the statement is not

Before the fields, the constraints. The statement is not a legal contract, not a warranty, and not an audit report. It is a published self-assessment. It carries no marketing language, because a statement that reads as promotion is already drifting toward the laundering HX-15 describes. And it is dated: it is true as of its review date and no later, which means it must be re-filed when the deployment changes materially.

# The seven fields

A Part H Statement carries seven things and nothing else.

- **Declared role.** Which of the four roles the deployer occupies — Author, Integrator, Operator, Commissioner — or the union where several apply. This field decides which duties are even in scope.
- **Declared scale tier.** The tier under §H.2-bis, by distinct persons affected in a twelve-month period, plus a note if a sensitive domain — liberty, livelihood, healthcare, benefits, minors — forces the top tier regardless of headcount.
- **Harms judged applicable.** Each applicable harm, one line each, naming the harm and how the deployment engages it. Brevity is the discipline; a line, not an essay.
- **Harms judged inapplicable.** Each inapplicable harm with the safe-harbour limb relied on — not a bare assertion of inapplicability, but the stated reason it does not attach.
- **Harms applicable and unremedied.** Any harm the deployer judges applicable but has not remedied, with a date. This is the field that costs an insincere adopter what they were trying to avoid, and costs a sincere one nothing.
- **The unnamed-harm review (§H.21).** A short statement naming any structural harm of the deployment the register does not reach, or an affirmative statement that the review found none, with method.
- **Date and version.** The review date and the statement version, so a reader knows how current it is.

# The worked instance — the smallest honest statement

A two-person team below ten thousand persons owes the least: the seven absolutes and a one-page statement. Their statement might declare role Author-and-Operator, tier one, list HX-03 as applicable-and-remedied by labelling machine output, list HX-01 and HX-04 as inapplicable because the tool takes no advertising and derives no revenue from duration, run the unnamed-harm review and find none, and carry a date. That is a complete, compliant statement in well under a page — which is the point. The Compliance group's generator assembles exactly this from a few answers.

# Limits of the form

The form structures honesty; it cannot enforce it. A statement is only as truthful as the answers behind it, and the license does not review or store it — publication is the deployer's own act. The inapplicability field requires naming a real safe harbour, but the deployer judges whether the safe harbour is met. And the form is one page by design: a deployer with more to say files it elsewhere and links it, because a statement that runs to many pages has stopped being the single surface the license needs.

# The falsifier for this page

The form's governing claim is that a single one-page statement makes Part H compliable where the full register alone would not. The refutation: if licensees given this form comply no more completely than those given only the register, the form adds a surface without adding compliance.

# What the form obliges

Fill every field, especially the unremedied-harms field — the omission of an unmet harm is itself the harm. Publish at a stable URL, because a statement that cannot be found is not published. And read the estate's own statement as the worked example that matters most: it names two absolutes unremedied on its own front-door instrument, which is the form doing exactly what it exists to do.
