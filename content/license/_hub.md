# The instrument the whole universe is built on

The License is the governing instrument of the MetaX universe, and it is the soul of the estate rather than a footer link. Every other pillar operates under the Maher License 2.3, and the estate holds itself to the License in public — including, and especially, where it currently falls short. ML-2.3's defining contribution is Part H: a register of twenty-five structural harms of computing and networked order, each stated in a form a stranger can attack, made operative by seven absolute prohibitions and twenty-seven duties, and gated by proportionality so that the smallest actors owe the least. The pillar's five groups are the Instrument, the Register, Operative Machinery, Compliance, and Falsification & History.

## Why this pillar exists

Most licenses govern copying. ML-2.3 governs conduct, because the characteristic injury of computing is not unauthorised copying but structural harm — harm that no actor intends, that arises from how a system is built and financed rather than from malice, that is distributed so thinly across so many people that no single instance is worth acting on, and that is invisible to the party causing it because the metric by which that party judges itself is the very instrument of the harm. The four ethical grounds of the earlier license — deception, harm, oppression, betrayal of trust — are written for conduct and do not reach that class. Part H closes the hole in the middle of the instrument by naming the structural harms directly and attaching to each a mechanism, published evidence including evidence against, a falsifier, and a design decision a named role can actually make.

## The map — what lives inside License

**The Instrument** is ML-2.3 itself, part by part: Part 0 preamble and standing, Part A definitions, Part B grant, Part C ethical conditions, Part D notices and disclosure, Part E governance, Part F breach and termination, Part G interpretation, Part H structural harms, the five schedules, and a plain-text mirror that stays legible with CSS disabled. **The Register** presents the twenty-five harms by their six strata — Cognitive, Relational, Economic, Material, Reflexive, and Successor — plus all falsifiers. **Operative Machinery** is how Part H bites: the seven absolutes of §H.3, the roles of §H.1-bis, the scale gate of §H.2-bis, the condition-versus-covenant distinction of §F.4, cure and reinstatement, and the four elements of Riba under §C.9. **Compliance** is the surface a licensee actually uses: the Statement Form of Schedule 1, a statement generator, MetaX's own statement, worked examples by tier, and compatibility and SPDX. **Falsification & History** is the license's own honesty layer: objections and responses, withdrawals and narrowings, the defect log, the changelog from 2.2 to 2.3, the superseded ML-2.2, and the open questions.

## How Part H is meant to work

Part H does not ask a licensee to read twenty-five essays and twenty-seven clauses before shipping. It resolves to a single artefact — the Part H Statement — a one-page, published document naming the licensee's declared role, its scale tier, which harms it judges applicable, which it judges inapplicable and the safe harbour it relies on, any harm it judges applicable and unremedied with a date, and the unnamed-harm review. Below ten thousand persons affected, a deployer owes only that statement and the seven absolutes. The scale gate rises from there: the largest deployments, and any deployment touching liberty, livelihood, healthcare, benefits, or minors at any scale, owe everything, published. The absolutes never scale down, because that is what makes them absolute.

## The worked frame — the estate's own statement

The estate applies Part H to itself, and the result is uncomfortable on purpose. MetaX's own Part H Statement must currently mark two harms as unremedied — HX-01, attention expropriation, and HX-04, the human as product — because the content plane carries advertising slots, and an advertising-funded surface engages exactly the mechanisms those two harms describe. Since the site is a demo, there is a clean window to remove the ad slots before the statement is ever published, which would let the disposition honestly read remedied; but if the ads stay, the honest text stays, and it sits two clicks from the license the estate asks others to adopt. The estate defaults to the spec as written: the statement marks HX-01 and HX-04 unremedied, with the ad slots named as the reason and the removal offered as the remedy.

## Limits of this pillar

The License is drafted by a curator who is explicitly not a lawyer and not a scholar; the condition-versus-covenant line, the enforceability of use restrictions, and the Islamic-ethics mappings are all flagged as needing review by counsel and by a qualified scholar before ML-2.3 issues, and that review status is printed rather than assumed. Part H creates no third-party right of action: an affected person is not a party to the license. The register is a floor, not a ceiling — its own non-exhaustiveness clause states that a harm is not authorised by its absence from the list. And one defect stays open by design: the bibliography must be assembled by a human with the sources in front of them, because a machine-generated reference list in a document whose whole claim is checkable evidence would be the very harm the register names.

## The falsifier for the pillar

Part H's governing claim is that naming structural harms operatively, with falsifiers and scaled duties, changes conduct more than a general good-faith clause does. The refutation: if published Part H Statements predict no measurable reduction in the harms they name, at effect sizes distinguishable from selection, then Part H is admired rather than obeyed — which is HX-15 conduct by the instrument itself — and it must be replaced with enforceable structure.

## What this pillar asks of you

If you build on the estate or adopt the License, write your Part H Statement early and publish your adverse findings; a party that has never published a finding against itself is, by the register's own test, doing compliance theatre. Read the seven absolutes first, because they bind you irrespective of scale. And treat the estate's own unremedied HX-01 and HX-04 marks as the point rather than an embarrassment: an instrument that could not name its own failures would not be worth adopting.
