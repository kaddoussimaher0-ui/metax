# Why seven of twenty-five harms are absolute

The seven absolutes are the prohibitions Part H refuses to soften for anyone. Everything else in the register is a duty — a covenant that a licensee can breach and cure — but these seven are conditions on the grant itself: use outside them is unlicensed. The ratio is deliberate and defended in the drafting. A register in which everything is absolute is one in which nothing is, because a licensee facing twenty-five sweeping bans learns that the instrument is unusable and ignores all of it. Twenty-eight percent absolute, seventy-two percent covenant, is the estate's judgement of the line between what must never be traded away and what can be managed by disclosure and cure.

# What makes a harm absolute rather than a duty

An absolute is not the worst harm; it is the harm for which no disclosure, no scale, and no cure can make the conduct acceptable. The test the register applies: could a party do this and remain within the license by disclosing it, accounting for it, or curing it within ninety days? Where the answer is no — where the conduct is wrong at any scale and cannot be remedied after the fact — the harm is an absolute. Where the answer is yes — where disclosure or a stopping rule genuinely changes the moral situation — the harm is a covenant.

# The seven, in causal order

The seven absolutes sit at §H.3(a)(i) through (vii). **(i) HX-01, attention expropriation** — bounded to duration-or-frequency engagement optimisation, not ordinary usefulness. **(ii) HX-04, the human as product** — bounded to making a person the good transacted without a disclosed, priceable account. **(iii) HX-06, algorithmic governance without due process** — bounded to consequential decisions made with no reason, appeal, or answerable party. **(iv) HX-03, synthetic provenance** — bounded to misrepresenting generated content as recorded fact. **(v) HX-14, moral deskilling in part** — bounded to consequential determinations with no named human author of record. **(vi) HX-24, developmental capture** — bounded to engagement optimisation or profiling against a person the operator knows is a minor, without ceasing on notice. **(vii) HX-15, false compliance representation** — bounded to representing compliance as evidence a named harm is absent where no assessment was made.

# The worked instance — why each absolute is bounded

The estate's drafting principle is that a bounded absolute is stronger than an unbounded one, and §H.3(a)(vii) is the clearest case. It does not ban adopting ethics instruments, and it does not require proving effect — either would fail, and deserve to. It carries exactly three cumulative elements: a representation to a person, that a Work complies, made as evidence a named harm is absent, where the representing party conducted no assessment. A party outside any one of those elements is outside the absolute. That precision is what makes it enforceable: a licensee can read it and know whether they are in breach, which an unbounded "do not launder ethics" prohibition could never deliver.

# Limits of these absolutes

The absolutes bind only within their cumulative elements — a system deriving no revenue from duration is outside HX-01 regardless of how engaging it is, because engagement alone is not the harm. They attach on the knowledge standard §H.3(b) sets: actual knowledge for the absolutes, which is why §H.3(a)(vi) is drafted around notice rather than around age verification. And whether they operate as true conditions on the grant is jurisdiction-sensitive and flagged for counsel review; the estate states its reading rather than asserting a ruling.

# The falsifier for this page

The governing claim is that a small set of bounded absolutes is obeyed where a large set of sweeping ones is ignored. The refutation: if licensees facing seven bounded absolutes comply no better than those facing twenty-five unbounded prohibitions, the bounding adds precision without changing conduct, and the ratio should be revisited.

# What these absolutes oblige

Read all seven regardless of your scale tier, because the scale gate does not reduce them — that is what absolute means. Check each against its cumulative elements to see whether it attaches to you. And take the estate's own example seriously: its statement marks two absolutes, HX-01 and HX-04, unremedied, because the ad slots put it inside their elements — the honest response to an unmet absolute is to name it, not to argue the elements away.
