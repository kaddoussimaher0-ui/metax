# The machinery that turns a register into an enforceable instrument

This group holds the parts of Part H that make it bite. A register of harms is a reading of the world; the machinery is what converts that reading into obligations a named party owes and a breach the license can respond to. Without it, Part H would be an essay. With it, a licensee can determine, in one reading, which role they occupy, which tier they fall in, which duties bind them, which are absolute, and what happens if they breach. The machinery is deliberately proportionate: it imposes the heaviest burden on the actors with the greatest capacity to cause the harms, and the lightest on the smallest.

## The four pieces of machinery

**Roles (§H.1-bis)** name the four parties Part H addresses — Author, who creates or modifies the Work; Integrator, who embeds it in a product; Operator, who runs a deployment against real persons; and Commissioner, who procures a deployment and sets its objective. Every duty states which role it binds, because a duty that binds "a builder" is unenforceable when the author and the operator are different parties, which is the ordinary case in all licensed software. **The Scale Gate (§H.2-bis)** sets three tiers by persons affected: below ten thousand, the absolutes plus a one-page Part H Statement; between ten thousand and a million, absolutes plus disclosure duties in full; above a million, or any deployment touching liberty, livelihood, healthcare, benefits, or minors at any scale, everything published. **The Absolutes (§H.3(a))** are the seven prohibitions that never scale down. **The breach machinery (§F.4 / §H.3(b))** states that the absolutes operate as conditions on the grant — use outside them is unlicensed — while every other duty is a covenant, breach of which is cured within ninety days or annotated in the registry.

## The map — what lives in this group

Each mechanism is a leaf page. **The Seven Absolutes (§H.3)** states the prohibitions that bind irrespective of scale. **Roles (§H.1-bis)** defines the four parties and shows how duties attach. **The Scale Gate (§H.2-bis)** states the three tiers and what each owes. **Conditions vs Covenants (§F.4)** explains what breach does and why the distinction is dispositive. **Cure & Reinstatement** states the ninety-day window and when cure is possible. **Riba — the Four Elements (§C.9)** presents the one absolute prohibition the license carries from its earlier versions, bounded by four cumulative elements.

## The worked frame — why an unbounded prohibition is weaker

The estate's drafting principle is that a bounded absolute is stronger than an unbounded one. Core Riba is prohibited absolutely, but the prohibition is bounded by four cumulative elements, so a licensee can tell exactly what is forbidden. The same logic governs the seven Part H absolutes: each is bounded by cumulative elements rather than stated as a sweeping ban. An unbounded prohibition is not stronger; it is unenforceable, and an unenforceable prohibition teaches licensees that the whole instrument may be ignored — which is the failure the register's own method warns against.

## Limits of this group

The condition-versus-covenant line is jurisdiction-sensitive and is flagged for counsel review before the license issues. The scale gate measures persons affected in a twelve-month period, which requires the deployer to estimate honestly — the machinery cannot verify the estimate, only require it. And the absolutes bind only where their cumulative elements are all met; a party outside those elements is not in breach, which is the point of bounding them.

## The falsifier for this group

The governing claim is that role-attached, scale-gated duties are enforceable where undifferentiated ones are not. The refutation: if disputes under Part H turn out to be no more resolvable with the roles and tiers than without them, the machinery adds complexity without enforceability and should be simplified.

## What this group asks of you

Determine your role and your tier before you read any duty, because both decide what you owe. Read the absolutes regardless of tier — they never scale down. And if you breach a covenant, the cure window is ninety days: the Compliance group's statement generator is the fastest way to document that you have cured.
