# Why Part H needs a proportionality gate

Read literally and without a gate, Part H would impose the heaviest burden on the actors least able to cause the harms it names. A two-person team shipping an educational tool would owe a published worst-future-holder assessment, a dependency map with single points of failure, energy and water figures with method, a plurality of metrics, and a measured fallback-competence interval — for a deployment reaching a few hundred people. That is the exact failure the register warns against: an instrument so demanding that licensees learn it may be ignored, imposing the most on those with the least capacity, since every harm in the first four strata is a harm of scale. The scale gate at §H.2-bis fixes this by making the duties proportionate to the persons affected.

# How the gate measures scale

Scale is measured as **distinct natural persons subject to the deployment's outputs in any twelve-month period** — not revenue, not headcount, not funding. This measure tracks the thing the harms track: exposure. A deployer estimates it honestly and declares the tier in their Part H Statement; the gate cannot verify the estimate, only require it, which is why the declaration is itself part of the compliance surface.

# The three tiers

**Below 10,000 persons.** The seven absolutes apply in full, and all other duties reduce to a single obligation: publish a one-page Part H Statement naming which harms the deployer judges applicable and why. Nothing else. This is the tier most licensees occupy, and it is where the estate itself declares. **Between 10,000 and 1,000,000 persons.** The absolutes plus the disclosure duties apply in full; the assessment duties — the worst-future-holder assessment of §H.9, the dependency map of §H.15 — are required but may be internal and produced on request rather than published. **Above 1,000,000 persons, or any deployment touching liberty, livelihood, healthcare, benefits, or minors at any scale.** Everything applies, and everything is published. The sensitive-domain override is the important part of the top tier: a benefits-eligibility system reaching five thousand people is in the top tier regardless of its size, because the stakes, not the headcount, decide.

# The worked instance — the estate's own tier declaration

MetaX.Academy declares the lowest tier in its own Part H Statement, below ten thousand distinct persons affected, because the live site is a demo with limited traffic. At that tier it owes the seven absolutes and a one-page statement — which is exactly what it files. The estate nonetheless completes the fuller disclosure a higher tier would require, not because the gate compels it, but because it authors the register and cannot credibly ask larger deployers to disclose what it will not. The gate sets the floor; a deployer may always exceed it.

# Limits of the gate

The gate softens the duties; it never softens the absolutes, because a gate that reduced the absolutes would hollow §H.3(a) — the point of an absolute is that it holds at every scale. The persons-affected measure requires an honest estimate the instrument cannot audit. And the sensitive-domain override rests on the deployer correctly classifying its own domain, which a deployer motivated to stay in a lower tier may get wrong; the register treats a misclassification as a covenant breach, curable but recorded.

# The falsifier for this page

The gate's governing claim is that proportionate duties are complied with where flat ones are ignored, without letting large deployers escape the obligations their scale warrants. The refutation: if small deployers comply no better under the gate than under flat duties, or if the gate lets large deployers systematically under-declare, the gate has failed at one of its two jobs and must be recalibrated.

# What the gate obliges

Estimate your persons-affected honestly and declare your tier in your statement. Check the sensitive-domain override before you settle on a tier — liberty, livelihood, healthcare, benefits, or minors put you in the top tier at any size. And remember that the gate never touches the seven absolutes: whatever your tier, read them and check each against its elements, as the estate did when it found itself inside HX-01 and HX-04.
