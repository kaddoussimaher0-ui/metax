# What the cognitive stratum injures, and why it comes first

Stratum I is placed first because the faculty it protects — the capacity for sustained, deliberate reasoning — is the one every other stratum's defence depends on. A person whose attention has been expropriated cannot notice the surveillance of Stratum II, evaluate the metric colonisation of Stratum III, or contest the successor-foreclosure of Stratum VI. The three cognitive harms answer a single question that Part C's four conduct grounds could not reach: what does an attention-funded, recommendation-mediated, generative internet do to the mind of the person using it, structurally, whether or not any actor intends it. The register grounds this stratum in *Ḥifẓ al-ʿAql*, the preservation of the intellect — a reasoned extension of a classical principle, filed as an open question rather than asserted as a ruling.

# HX-01 — Attention Expropriation

The thesis is that systems funded by duration or frequency of engagement are built to capture attention against the interest of the person whose attention it is, and that the capture is a structural property of the funding model rather than a choice by any operator. The mechanism is variable-ratio reinforcement — the same schedule that makes slot machines effective — applied to feeds, notifications, and recommendation, tuned continuously against a measured response the user cannot see. The evidence is contested and the register says so: the strongest published critiques of the attention literature are reproduced rather than paraphrased, because a harm stated without its counter is a doctrine. What survives the counter is structural: a person cannot compare the appeal they received with what others received, and cannot see the instrumentation tuned against them.

HX-01 is one of the seven absolutes, at §H.3(a)(i), bounded so that it attaches to duration-or-frequency engagement optimisation and not to ordinary usefulness. Its safe harbour withdraws the harm from any system deriving no revenue from duration. Its falsifier: a well-powered adversarial replication showing that variable-ratio scheduling produces no persistence effect would retire it. **This is one of the two harms MetaX's own statement currently marks unremedied,** because the estate's content plane carries advertising slots that engage exactly this mechanism.

# HX-02 — Cognitive Offloading and Deskilling

The thesis is that delegating a cognitive task to a system does not merely assist the faculty; over time it atrophies it, and the atrophy is invisible because the delegated output looks the same whether or not the underlying skill survives. The mechanism is the justification asymmetry: following the system's suggestion requires nothing, departing from it requires a written reason, and the asymmetry eliminates the practice through which the skill is maintained. The evidence draws on the human-factors literature on automation bias. The strongest counter is that offloading is how every tool works, and that literacy itself offloaded memory to writing — a real objection, answered by distinguishing offloading that frees a faculty for higher work from offloading that removes the faculty entirely.

HX-02 is not absolute; it generates a disclosure-and-retention duty. Its safe harbour is a maintained practice interval in which the skill is exercised without assistance. Its falsifier: evidence that assisted practitioners retain the underlying skill at rates equal to unassisted ones would retire it.

# HX-03 — Synthetic Provenance and the Collapse of the Record

The thesis is that when content can be generated indistinguishably from recorded fact, the ability to tell what happened from what was fabricated collapses — not for any single item but for the record as a whole, so that even true content loses its evidentiary standing. The mechanism is model collapse: generated content enters the corpus, is trained on, and progressively displaces the recorded base, narrowing what is expressible and citable. The evidence is the documented degradation of models trained on their own output. The strongest counter is that provenance has always been contestable and that the corpus does not authorise anyone to adjudicate truth — which the estate accepts, and which is why the duty is disclosure of synthesis, not suppression of content.

HX-03 is one of the seven absolutes, at §H.3(a)(iv), bounded to the misrepresentation of generated content as recorded fact. Its safe harbour is persistent labelling of machine-generated material so it can be excluded from future corpora. Its falsifier: evidence that unlabelled synthetic content does not degrade the evidentiary standing of the surrounding record would retire it.

# The mechanism these three share

All three cognitive harms share one structure: the injured faculty is the one that would have noticed the injury. Expropriated attention cannot attend to its own expropriation; a deskilled judgement cannot judge its own decay; a collapsed record cannot record its own collapse. This is why the stratum sits first and why its ethical grounding in *Ḥifẓ al-ʿAql* is load-bearing rather than decorative — the harm is to the instrument of self-defence itself.

# The worked instance — the estate's own advertising slots

The estate applies HX-01 to itself and fails the test, on purpose and in public. MetaX's content plane carries AdSense and Adsterra slots, and an advertising-funded surface derives revenue from engagement, which is precisely the mechanism HX-01 names. The estate's Part H Statement therefore marks HX-01 unremedied, dated, with the ad slots named as the reason and their removal offered as the remedy. Because the site is a demo, the estate has a clean window to remove the slots and let the disposition read remedied; unless and until it does, the honest text stays, two clicks from the license the estate asks others to adopt.

# Limits of this stratum

This stratum reaches the mind, not the market or the material world — those are Strata III and IV. Its evidence is the most contested in the register, particularly for HX-01, and the entries carry that contestation openly rather than resolving it. And its absolutes bind only within their bounded elements: a system that derives no revenue from duration is outside HX-01 regardless of how engaging it is, because engagement alone is not the harm.

# The falsifier for the stratum

The stratum's governing claim is that attention-funded, generative systems structurally injure the faculty of sustained reasoning. The refutation: if well-powered longitudinal studies show that heavy users of such systems retain attention, skill, and the ability to judge provenance at rates indistinguishable from light users, the stratum's core premise fails and its harms reduce to their safe-harbour limbs alone.

# What this stratum obliges

If you deploy an attention-funded surface, you owe an honest HX-01 disposition in your Part H Statement — and if you cannot mark it remedied, you owe the reason and a date. If you build a tool that offloads a cognitive task, you owe a disclosure and a retained-practice path under HX-02. If you generate content, you owe persistent labelling under HX-03. The estate's own unremedied HX-01 is the model: name the failure rather than hide it.
