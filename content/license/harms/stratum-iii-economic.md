# What the economic stratum injures

Stratum III reaches the economic order — the structure of markets, labour, measurement, and access — rather than any individual dealing. Its four harms share a feature: each concentrates a benefit and distributes a cost so thinly that no single instance is actionable, which is the defining signature of structural harm. The stratum groups HX-07, HX-08, and HX-09 with HX-13, exclusion by default, whose remedy is channel parity rather than the exit-and-portability remedy the other three share. It is grounded in *Adl*, proportional justice, and in the trusteeship norms of *istikhlāf*.

# HX-07 — Centralisation and the Single Point of Governance

The thesis is that networked systems tend toward winner-take-most concentration, and that concentration converts a market into a private government whose subjects have no vote. The mechanism is network effects: value rises with adopters, migration cost rises faster, and lock-in follows. The evidence is the documented concentration of platform markets. The strongest counter — reproduced rather than dismissed — is that apparently locked-in platforms have repeatedly been displaced, so foreclosure claims have a poor track record. The reply narrows the duty to disclosure of single points of governance and to preserving exit, not to a ban on scale.

HX-07 is not absolute; it generates a disclosure-and-exit duty. Its safe harbour is a published dependency map and a working export path. Its falsifier: evidence that within-market migration costs are linear rather than superlinear in adoption would retire it.

# HX-08 — Extractive Labour and the Deactivation Asymmetry

The thesis is that platform-mediated labour extracts value while disclaiming the duties of employment, and that the worker can be deactivated by a system with no appeal — the labour equivalent of HX-06. The mechanism is classification arbitrage plus algorithmic management. The evidence is the gig-economy and content-moderation literature. The strongest counter is that flexible work is preferred by many who do it — answered by narrowing the duty to a deactivation appeal, not to reclassification.

HX-08 is not absolute; it binds the Operator to provide a deactivation appeal. Its safe harbour is a stated ground and a route of appeal before deactivation. Its falsifier: evidence that deactivated workers obtain remedy at rates comparable to appealable dismissals would retire it.

# HX-09 — Metric Colonisation

The thesis is that when a single metric becomes the objective, it displaces the plural goods it was meant to proxy, and the institution optimises the number while the thing the number measured decays — Goodhart's law as a structural harm. The mechanism is that a measurable proxy attracts the optimisation budget and the unmeasured goods starve. The evidence is the well-documented literature on target displacement. The strongest counter is that measurement is indispensable — answered by requiring a plurality of non-substitutable metrics and a published statement of what they omit, not by abandoning measurement.

HX-09 is not absolute; it binds the Operator to metric honesty. Its safe harbour is a plurality of metrics with a stated account of what they cannot see. Its falsifier: evidence that single-metric optimisation does not displace unmeasured goods would retire it.

# HX-13 — Exclusion by Default

The thesis is that where digital provision becomes the sole channel to a right or necessity, the right is withdrawn from everyone who cannot reach the channel, and the withdrawal is never decided — it happens when the last non-digital path is retired for efficiency. The mechanism is channel retirement costed against the average user, never the marginal one. The evidence includes documented benefit-access failures under digital-by-default administration. The strongest counter is that digital channels expand access far more than they remove it — accepted as to net effect; the objection is to retiring the last alternative for a functionally compulsory service.

HX-13 is not absolute; it binds Operator and Commissioner to channel parity. Its safe harbour is a maintained non-digital channel of equal entitlement plus WCAG 2.2 AA conformance with published gaps. Its falsifier: evidence that a digital-only channel achieves equal realised access across every excluded population would retire it.

# The mechanism these harms share

All four concentrate benefit and distribute cost below the threshold of individual action. That is why they are scale-gated: the largest deployers, who cause the harms at scale, owe published assessments, while the smallest owe only a statement. It is also why HX-13's remedy differs — its injury is a withdrawal of access, so its remedy is parity of channel, not exit and portability.

# The worked instance — the estate's own exclusion defect

The estate applies HX-13 to itself and records a defect: its social-only sign-in providers exclude anyone unwilling or unable to use a listed provider, and serve the Arabic-market audience worse than the English one. This is filed as both an HX-13 exclusion and an HX-25 linguistic-flattening concern in the estate's own statement, rather than left unnamed — the unnamed-harm review at work.

# Limits of this stratum

This stratum reaches the economic order, not the mind (Stratum I) or the material world (Stratum IV). Its duties are heavily scale-gated, because the harms are harms of scale and imposing the full burden on a two-person team would be the exact over-demand the register warns against.

# The falsifier for the stratum

The stratum's governing claim is that networked economic systems structurally concentrate benefit and distribute unactionable cost. The refutation: if concentration, deactivation, metric displacement, and channel retirement produce no distributed cost beyond what a competitive market would, the stratum's premise fails.

# What this stratum obliges

If you operate at scale, publish the dependency map (HX-07), the deactivation appeal (HX-08), the plural metrics and their blind spots (HX-09), and the channel-parity provision (HX-13). If you are small, name which apply in your statement and why. And run the unnamed-harm review honestly, as the estate did to surface its own exclusion defect.
