# Why the register needed a stratum about itself

Stratum V is the register turning on itself. Its five harms are meta-harms in the strict sense: their subject matter is the harm-governance apparatus, including this register and this License. A register that did not include them would be structurally unable to detect its own capture — and capture is the most predictable way an instrument like this fails, not by being violated but by being *complied with* while the harm continues. The stratum was added because Strata I–IV classify harms by what they injure in the world, and none of them reaches an injury to the capacity to perceive, name, contest, and remedy harm. It is grounded in *Sidq*, *Amanah*, and *Ḥifẓ al-ʿAql* at the level of an institution rather than a mind.

# HX-15 — Ethics Laundering and Compliance Theatre

The thesis is that adopting an ethics instrument reduces external scrutiny faster than it reduces harm, so adopting the instrument becomes the most cost-effective way to continue the conduct it names. The damaging step is the fourth: the published commitment becomes admissible evidence of good faith, worth more than the harm costs. The evidence is the convergence of over eighty AI-ethics principle sets onto abstractions with no observed effect on deployment, and the ESG disclosure-versus-outcome gap. The strongest counter is that this harm is unfalsifiable in a dangerous way — it lets a critic dismiss any adopter, including a sincere one. The reply is decisive: the obligation is not to prove effect but to publish adverse findings, and the distinguishing test is whether the adopter has ever published a finding against itself — cheap to check, hard to fake.

HX-15 is one of the seven absolutes in part, at §H.3(a)(vii): a party may not represent compliance as evidence that a named harm is absent where it has conducted no assessment. Plus §H.20, the non-laundering and adverse-findings duty. Falsifier: evidence that published ethics commitments predict measured harm reductions would retire it. **This is the harm the estate's own statement exists to answer.**

# HX-16 — Register Closure and the Named-Harm Ceiling

The thesis is that a finite, authoritative list of harms becomes a ceiling rather than a floor — the unnamed harm becomes arguably permitted, because enumeration invites the inference that what is not listed was considered and cleared. The evidence is the "not a protected class, therefore lawful" pattern in anti-discrimination law and safety-standard compliance operating as a liability defence. The counter is that the alternative to enumeration is a vague general standard, which is unenforceable — accepted in full, which is why the remedy is not to abandon enumeration but to add three cheap provisions: an express non-exhaustiveness clause, a residual test, and an annual unnamed-harm review.

HX-16 is not absolute; it binds all roles (scaled) via §H.21, which carries the clause that nothing in Part H authorises structural harm by reason of its absence from the register. Falsifier: evidence that enumerated-harm regimes do not produce implied-negative reasoning would retire it.

# HX-17 — Semantic Capture

The thesis is that whoever controls the meaning of the words in which a harm must be alleged controls whether it can be alleged at all — the redefinition of the vocabulary of accountability by the parties it is used to hold accountable. The evidence is "open" applied to restricted-weight models, "privacy" narrowed to transport encryption, "consent" reduced to dismissing a banner. The counter is that language always changes and prescriptivism loses — answered by the observation that this is not drift but *asymmetric* drift under interest, always toward the incumbent's existing conduct. The remedy is not to fix meanings but to require disclosure of the definition in use.

HX-17 is not absolute; §H.22 binds Author, Integrator, and Operator: no normatively loaded term may be applied to a Work without a published definition in use. Falsifier: evidence that contested-term drift is directionally random with respect to interest would retire it.

# HX-18 — Remedy Foreclosure

The thesis is that the most efficient response to a harm is not to reduce it but to remove the forum in which it could be raised — through mandatory individual arbitration, class waiver, unilateral amendment, forum selection, and anti-measurement terms. The evidence is the documented collapse in filed claims following arbitration adoption. The counter is that arbitration can serve individual claimants better and anti-scraping terms have real anti-abuse purposes — accepted, which is why the surviving duty is narrow: preserve aggregate redress for register harms, no retroactive amendment, and a safe harbour for good-faith measurement.

HX-18 is not absolute; §H.23 binds Operator, Commissioner, and the Steward — including the License's own dispute provisions. Falsifier: evidence that populations under mandatory arbitration obtain remedy for distributed harms at comparable rates would retire it.

# HX-19 — Interpretive Concentration

The thesis is that an instrument whose meaning is determined by a single party has no meaning independent of that party's continued good faith, competence, and existence. This is the register's sharpest internal tension, because the estate argues elsewhere that single authorship is a feature at its scale. It resolves by distinguishing authorship from interpretation: HX-14 requires a named author, not a sole reader. The remedy is not a committee but three provisions — publish determinations with reasoning and rejected alternatives, publish dissent, and void unpublished determinations after a stated period.

HX-19 is not absolute; §H.24 binds the Steward of the License. Falsifier: evidence that single-steward instruments interpret no less predictably and no more self-servingly than distributed ones, across at least one steward transition, would retire it. **The estate discloses its residual exposure here: MetaX has one curator, and this harm is currently mitigated by disclosure rather than resolved by structure.**

# The mechanism these harms share

All five attack the second-order capacity to govern harm: HX-15 corrupts the instrument's signal, HX-16 corrupts its scope, HX-17 corrupts its vocabulary, HX-18 corrupts its forum, HX-19 corrupts its interpretation. A register blind to them would be a register that could be perfectly complied with while failing entirely.

# The worked instance — this document

The estate is Stratum V's own worked example. Its public defect register (against HX-15), its two added strata and three-per-cycle amendment cap (against HX-16), its contested-terms lexicon (against HX-17), its MX-016 appeals and public queue (against HX-18), and its published determinations with a disclosed single-curator residual (against HX-19) are all this stratum in force. Where the estate cannot resolve a harm — HX-19 in particular — it discloses the exposure rather than claiming a structure it does not have.

# Limits of this stratum

This stratum reaches the governance apparatus, not the world it governs. None of its harms is fully absolute, because each remedy is disclosure or process rather than prohibition — a reflexive harm cannot be fixed by a ban without becoming the censorship HX-17 warns against.

# The falsifier for the stratum

The stratum's governing claim is that harm-governance apparatuses are themselves subject to capture, and that a register blind to this fails silently. The refutation: if instruments without these five provisions prove no more prone to laundering, closure, capture, foreclosure, or concentration than instruments with them, the stratum is unnecessary.

# What this stratum obliges

Publish your adverse findings (HX-15). Run an unnamed-harm review (HX-16). Disclose your definitions (HX-17). Preserve the forum (HX-18). Publish your determinations and dissent, and disclose any interpretive concentration you cannot resolve (HX-19) — as the estate does with its single-curator exposure.
