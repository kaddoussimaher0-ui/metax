# The register of harms the license makes operative

This group is the operative content of Part H: the twenty-five structural harms of computing and networked order that ML-2.3 incorporates by reference. Each harm is stated in a fixed eight-part form — thesis, mechanism, evidence, strongest counter-argument, reply, ethical mapping, safe harbour, and falsifier — so that a harm is not a slogan but a claim a stranger can attack. The register is a floor, not a ceiling: its own non-exhaustiveness clause states that a harm is not authorised by its absence from the list, and a residual test lets a harm meeting the selection criteria be reached before it is named.

## Why the register has six strata

Harms are grouped by what they injure. **Stratum I — Cognitive** (HX-01 to HX-03) injures the mind: attention, the capacity for sustained reasoning, and the provenance of content. **Stratum II — Relational** (HX-04 to HX-06) injures the person in relation: the human treated as product, ambient surveillance, and algorithmic governance without due process. **Stratum III — Economic** (HX-07 to HX-09, and HX-13) injures the economic order: centralisation, extraction, metric colonisation, and exclusion by default. **Stratum IV — Material** (HX-10 to HX-12, and HX-14) injures the material and temporal world: extractive materiality, and the harms of memory, custody, and delegated judgement. **Stratum V — Reflexive** (HX-15 to HX-19) injures the capacity to perceive, name, contest, and remedy harm — these are meta-harms whose subject is the harm-governance apparatus itself. **Stratum VI — Successor** (HX-20 to HX-25) injures parties not present to object: successors, non-participants, and the developing person.

## The map — what lives in this group

Each stratum is a leaf page carrying its harms in full. **Stratum I · Cognitive** holds HX-01 through HX-03. **Stratum II · Relational** holds HX-04 through HX-06. **Stratum III · Economic** holds HX-07 through HX-09 and HX-13. **Stratum IV · Material** holds HX-10 through HX-12 and HX-14. **Stratum V · Reflexive** holds HX-15 through HX-19 — ethics laundering, register closure, semantic capture, remedy foreclosure, and interpretive concentration. **Stratum VI · Successor** holds HX-20 through HX-25 — irreversibility, the unconsented experiment, persuasion asymmetry, synthetic relation, developmental capture, and linguistic flattening. And **All Falsifiers** collects, on one page, the single observation that would retire each harm.

## The worked frame — why every entry carries a counter-argument

The register's most unusual feature is that every harm states its own strongest counter-argument before replying to it. This is not rhetorical balance; it is the estate's falsifiability discipline applied to the license itself. A harm stated without its counter is a doctrine, and the estate does not publish doctrine. HX-22, persuasion asymmetry, is the clearest instance: the register reproduces the objection that the harm shades into a demand for censorship, concedes the empirical case is weak, and then narrows the obligation to disclosure in four named domains — so the surviving duty costs an honest persuader nothing and does not depend on contested effect sizes.

## Limits of this group

The register is a reasoned instrument, not a ruling. Its evidence is stated with its weaknesses, and where a harm rests on a structural claim rather than a measured effect, the entry says so. Its ethical mappings are reasoned extensions by a non-scholar, filed as open questions. And it creates no third-party right of action: an affected person is not a party to the license, and the register does not pretend otherwise.

## The falsifier for this group

The register's governing claim is that harms stated with falsifiers and scaled duties change conduct better than a general good-faith clause. The refutation: if published Part H Statements predict no measurable reduction in the harms they name, the register is admired rather than obeyed — which is HX-15 committed by the register itself.

## What this group asks of you

Read the stratum that matches what your deployment touches, then read the falsifier for each harm in it — the falsifier tells you what the estate would accept as proof it is wrong. If you adopt the license, the register resolves for you into a single artefact, the Part H Statement, and the Compliance group shows you how to write it.
