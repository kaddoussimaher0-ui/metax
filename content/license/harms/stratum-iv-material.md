# What the material stratum injures

Stratum IV reaches the world that persists — the physical substrate of computation and the temporal record it produces. Its harms are slower than the others and harder to see because they accrue over time and across many actors. The stratum groups extractive materiality (HX-10) and two temporal harms of the record (HX-11 custody, HX-12 digital amnesia and its inverse) with HX-14, moral deskilling, which the register calls "the last harm and the one the other thirteen enable." It is grounded in *Isrāf* and *Tabdhīr* — extravagance and squandering — and in *istikhlāf*, trusteeship of the earth for those who come after.

# HX-10 — Extractive Materiality

The thesis is that the apparent immateriality of computing conceals a heavy material footprint — energy, water, minerals, and embodied carbon — whose cost falls on people and places distant from the benefit. The mechanism is that the footprint is denominated in a currency the user never sees. The evidence is the datacentre energy-and-water literature and the minerals-supply record. The strongest counter is that computing displaces more material activity than it consumes — a real efficiency argument, answered by requiring accounting, not abstention: a deployer who publishes energy, water, and embodied-carbon figures with method has discharged the duty.

HX-10 is not absolute; it binds the Operator to material accounting at scale. Its safe harbour is published figures with a stated method. Its falsifier: evidence that computing's material footprint is net-negative once displacement is counted, at the deployment's scale, would retire it.

# HX-11 & HX-12 — Custody Failure and Digital Amnesia and Its Inverse

The thesis of the paired harm is that a civilisation recording everything preserves nothing it chose to preserve and forgets nothing a person needs forgotten — two failures of one mechanism. The mechanism: format and platform dependence make the record readable only by the system that wrote it; custody is commercial, so preservation is a cost centre and deletion a default; there is no accession decision, so nothing is deliberately kept; and simultaneously nothing decays, so a person's worst recorded day stays first-ranked. The evidence is link-rot rates in scholarly and judicial citation, the loss of platform-hosted corpora on shutdown, and the persistence of resolved matters in search. The counter is that preservation has never been better resourced and the demand to forget is often a demand to conceal — conceded as to accountability records, which is why the duty is custody-and-horizon, not a deletion right.

These harms are not absolute; they bind the Operator to a custody-and-horizon duty (§H.17). Safe harbour: a published accession and retention policy, export in a documented format, and a stated horizon after which non-accountability records are de-indexed. Falsifier: evidence that platform-hosted records survive platform death at rates comparable to institutional archives would retire the amnesia limb.

# HX-14 — Moral Deskilling and Delegated Judgement

The thesis is that judgement delegated to a system does not relocate; it dissolves, and after a period of delegation no one in the institution can make the decision at all and no one is answerable for it — the faculty and the authorship decay together. The mechanism has four steps: recommendation becomes default; the default becomes the audit-safe choice, so departing requires a written reason while following requires none; the asymmetry eliminates the practice that maintains judgement; and the institution's memory of why a rule exists is lost. The result is responsibility diffusion by design — the system, the operator, the vendor, and the commissioner each disclaim blame. The evidence includes the *toeslagenaffaire* and Robodebt records, in which no individual official is identifiable as the author. The counter is that institutions always diffused responsibility — substantially accepted, which is why HX-14 is a temporal harm, distinguished by two disanalogies: a committee can be convened and asked, a decayed capability cannot; and a committee produces reasons, a scored default produces none.

HX-14 is one of the seven absolutes in part, at §H.3(a)(v), and binds the Operator to an authorship-of-record duty (§H.19). Safe harbour: a named human author of record for every consequential determination, retaining the practice by which the judgement is maintained. Falsifier: evidence that delegated judgement is maintained rather than decayed over the delegation interval would retire it.

# The mechanism these harms share

All four accrue over time and across actors, which makes them the hardest to notice and the easiest to disclaim. HX-14 is the linchpin: a deskilled institution cannot account for its materiality, cannot decide what to preserve, and cannot author the decisions the other harms require — which is why the register calls it the harm the other thirteen enable.

# The worked instance — the estate's own archive rule

The estate applies this stratum through its archive discipline: nothing is deleted, only superseded with a header, so a successor can reconstruct what was decided and why (HX-11/HX-12); the build output is plain files and forward-only plain SQL, so the estate's own reversal cost is bounded (touching HX-20); and every consequential determination in the estate has a named author in the public queue (HX-14). The estate does not yet publish material-footprint figures for its own hosting, which it should file as an open HX-10 item.

# Limits of this stratum

This stratum reaches the persistent world, not the market (Stratum III) or the governance apparatus (Stratum V). HX-14's absolute binds only within its elements — a determination with a named author and a retained practice is outside it. And the custody duty distinguishes accountability records, which must persist, from other records, which get a horizon.

# The falsifier for the stratum

The stratum's governing claim is that computing's material and temporal costs accrue structurally and are systematically disclaimed. The refutation: if material footprints prove net-negative, records prove durable, and delegated judgement proves maintained, the stratum's premise fails.

# What this stratum obliges

Publish material figures at scale (HX-10). Publish an accession, retention, and horizon policy (HX-11/HX-12). Name a human author of record for every consequential determination and retain the practice that maintains the judgement (HX-14). And keep an archive that supersedes rather than deletes, as the estate does.
