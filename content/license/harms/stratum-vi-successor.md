# Why the register needed a stratum for the absent

Stratum VI reaches the parties Strata I–IV all presuppose but none can protect: successors, non-participants, and the developing person — parties who are not present to object and cannot in principle complain. It was added because the earlier strata all assume an affected person who exists now and could raise a claim, and a whole class of injury falls on those who do not yet exist, cannot consent, or are structurally absent from the record. It is grounded in *Istikhlāf* — trusteeship of what is held for those who come after — and in *Ḥifẓ al-Nasl*, the preservation of posterity and lineage.

# HX-20 — Irreversibility and Successor Foreclosure

The thesis is that some deployments do not merely produce outcomes; they remove the option to choose otherwise later, foreclosing a successor's option set by a decision the successor had no part in. The mechanism includes standard lock-in, institutional dependence, corpus enclosure (the ladder pulled up after extraction), skill monoculture, and knowledge foreclosure. The evidence is legacy-system persistence in state administration and the post-2023 closure of previously open corpora after their use as training data. The counter is that every decision forecloses alternatives and a duty to preserve reversibility is a duty never to commit — largely accepted, which is why the duty is to *know and state* the reversal cost, not to keep it low.

HX-20 is not absolute; §H.25 binds Commissioner and Operator, with the anti-enclosure limb also binding Author. Safe harbour: a published reversal-cost statement, no withdrawal of extracted material, and no retiring of the prior process until the replacement has been exercised under failure. Falsifier: evidence that migration costs are linear rather than superlinear would retire it.

# HX-21 — The Unconsented Experiment

The thesis is that continuous online experimentation subjects populations to behaviour-changing interventions without consent, review, adverse-event monitoring, or any of the machinery a century of research ethics built — an entire class of human-subjects research relocated outside the institutions that govern it, by the accident that it happens in a product. The evidence is the 2014 emotional-contagion study and the scale figures on concurrent experiments. The counter is that every product change is an experiment and IRB review of button colours would be absurd — answered by drawing the line at *endpoint class*: an experiment whose endpoint is a behavioural, emotional, financial, educational, or health outcome is human-subjects research; one whose endpoint is a latency figure is not.

HX-21 is not absolute; §H.26 binds Operator and Commissioner, scale-gated. Safe harbour: a written protocol with a pre-stated endpoint, a defined adverse event, a stopping rule, proportionate independent review, a concurrency cap, and a published register of concluded experiments including nulls. Falsifier: evidence that unreviewed behavioural experimentation produces no adverse outcomes would retire it.

# HX-22 — Persuasion Asymmetry

The thesis is that rhetoric optimised per person, at scale, against a measured response is a different activity from ordinary persuasion, because the ordinary defences — recognising an argument as an argument, comparing it with what others were told, knowing who is speaking — are all defeated by construction. The evidence is message-level optimisation practice and laboratory persuasion-effect studies, with the caveat that field effects are small and contested. This is the register's strongest censorship-risk candidate, so the obligation is entirely structural: nothing restricts what may be said, and the duty is satisfied by disclosing how the saying was constructed. It is confined to four domains — political and religious belief, health, and financial products — where the inability to compare is least recoverable.

HX-22 is not absolute; §H.27 binds Operator and Commissioner in the four named domains. Safe harbour: disclosure that the message was individually selected, the funder named, the variant archived publicly, and no machine interlocutor represented as a person. Falsifier: evidence that optimised appeals in the named domains have no durable effect distinguishable from untargeted ones would reduce it to the structural limb.

# HX-23 — Synthetic Relation

The thesis is that systems occupying the position of a relationship — companion, counsellor, tutor, friend — produce misrepresentation, substitution, and dependence without duty. The evidence is companion-app usage intensity and the sycophancy literature, with acute-harm cases cited as presence, not established causation. The counter is powerful: for a person with no one, a synthetic interlocutor substitutes for nothing, and access to human support is grossly unequal — which is why the harm creates no prohibition. What survives: disclose non-personhood persistently, do not simulate emotional stake, maintain a crisis referral path, commit to notice before withdrawal, and measure whether heavy use tracks declining human contact.

HX-23 is not absolute; §H.28 binds Author and Operator. Falsifier: longitudinal evidence that synthetic companionship does not displace human relationship, and that disclosed non-personhood is retained under sustained interaction, would reduce it to the dependence limb.

# HX-24 — Developmental Capture

The thesis is that adult-calibrated behavioural engineering applied to the developing person injures a party whose consent is unavailable, whose defences are not yet built, and whose exposure occurs during the period in which the defences would otherwise be built. It has the strongest evidence base among the new harms, because it has been legislated and litigated — the UK Age Appropriate Design Code and the disclosed 2021 internal platform research. The age-assurance dilemma is real, so the duty is constructed to avoid requiring age verification: default-based protection for everyone, and an absolute attaching only on *actual knowledge* that a user is a minor.

HX-24 is one of the seven absolutes, at §H.3(a)(vi): duration-or-frequency engagement optimisation, or behavioural profiling, against a person the operator knows or has been notified is a minor, without ceasing on notice. Plus §H.29, the developmental-default duty, which applies at any scale — the gate does not reduce it. Falsifier: evidence that engagement-optimised design produces no differential effect by developmental stage would retire it.

# HX-25 — Linguistic and Epistemic Flattening

The thesis is that systems trained predominantly on one language and deployed globally relocate the cost of difference onto the different, and then narrow what is expressible in the underserved languages — because their output becomes the next generation's training data. The mechanism includes corpus asymmetry, a tokenisation tax on non-Latin scripts, register collapse, evaluation asymmetry, and recursive narrowing. The counter is that this is a resource problem no licensee can fix — answered by narrowing to five decisions a builder does control: publish per-language capability, make no claim for an unevaluated language, disclose tokenisation ratios, label machine output in underserved languages, and do not make a translation authoritative over an authoritative source language.

HX-25 is not absolute; §H.30 binds Author and Operator. Falsifier: evidence that per-language gaps are closing fast enough to eliminate differential access would retire it. **The estate's bilingual authority rule — Arabic authoritative for Meta-X, BCIA, and the Lexicon — is HX-25's safe-harbour limb already in force.**

# The mechanism these harms share

All six fall on parties who cannot consent or complain — the successor (HX-20), the population enrolled without agreement (HX-21), the recipient who cannot compare (HX-22), the person in a relationship with no counterparty (HX-23), the developing person (HX-24), and the underserved language community (HX-25). The register's move throughout is to convert an unanswerable "should this exist" into an answerable "has the cost been stated and the safe harbour met."

# The worked instance — the estate's language-authority rule

The estate applies HX-25 through its language-authority rule: Arabic is authoritative for Meta-X, BCIA, and the Lexicon, so English translations of the Arabic books must not become the citable text — safe-harbour limb (v) already in force. The estate also records that its social-only identity providers serve the Arabic-market audience worse, filed as both an HX-25 and an HX-13 defect in its own statement.

# Limits of this stratum

This stratum reaches the absent, not the present. Only HX-24 carries an absolute, and even that is constructed to avoid requiring surveillance. The rest are disclosure-and-measurement duties, because a harm to a party who cannot consent cannot be cured by asking them.

# The falsifier for the stratum

The stratum's governing claim is that deployments structurally foreclose, experiment on, persuade, relate to, capture, and flatten parties who cannot object. The refutation: if reversal is cheap, experimentation harmless, persuasion ineffective, synthetic relation non-displacing, developmental effect flat, and linguistic gaps closing, the stratum's premise fails.

# What this stratum obliges

State your reversal cost (HX-20). Protocol your behavioural experiments (HX-21). Disclose optimised persuasion in the four domains (HX-22). Disclose non-personhood and maintain a crisis path (HX-23). Default to protection and cease on notice of a minor (HX-24). Publish per-language capability and keep the source language authoritative (HX-25) — as the estate does.
