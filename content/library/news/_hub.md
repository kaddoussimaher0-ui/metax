# The estate's dated record of what changed and when

News is the group that carries the estate's dated dispatches — announcements, curriculum releases, research briefs, operator field notes, and the archive. It exists because an estate that changes needs a dated record of its changes, or a reader cannot tell what is new from what is settled. Unlike a marketing blog, the estate's news is held to the same disciplines as everything else: every item is dated, every claim carries its evidence class, and nothing is quietly edited after publication — a correction goes to the corrections log, not into a silent revision. Its five pages are announcements, curriculum releases, research briefs, operator field notes, and the archive.

## Why the news is disciplined like the rest of the estate

Most sites treat news as the one place where marketing language is allowed. The estate refuses the exemption: a research brief carries an evidence class, an operator field note states its decay interval, and a curriculum release links to the changelog entry that records it. This is the estate's method applied to its own dispatches — news is not where the standards relax, it is where dated claims about the estate itself are held to the same falsifiability and decay discipline as a course page. A brief that overclaimed would be as much a defect as an overclaiming lesson.

## The map — what lives in this group

**Announcements** carries dated estate announcements. **Curriculum Releases** records new and updated series and courses, linked to the catalog changelog. **Research Briefs** carries dated, evidence-classed summaries from the Meta-X and Ascent frontiers. **Operator Field Notes** carries dated notes from operator practice, each with its decay interval. **Archive** holds the full dated history, nothing deleted.

## The worked instance — a research brief with an evidence class

A research brief is the group's clearest instance of disciplined news. Rather than announce a "breakthrough," a brief states what was observed, its evidence class — typically low, given the research-preview pillars it summarises — and the falsifier that would overturn it. It reads as a dated question from the frontier, not a settled claim, which is exactly what the Ascent and Meta-X pillars' citation-only and research-preview disciplines require. A brief that laundered a speculative result into a certainty would be the successor-foreclosure harm the License names, committed in the news.

## Limits of this group

News is dated and decays like any claim; an old brief is a record of what was thought then, not a current position. The archive keeps everything, including superseded items, marked as such. And research briefs summarise research-preview work, so their evidence classes are deliberately low — a brief is a dispatch from a frontier, not a report from a laboratory.

## The falsifier for this group

The group's governing claim is that news held to the estate's disciplines — dated, evidence-classed, never silently edited — is more trustworthy than a marketing blog. The refutation: if readers of the estate's news are misled about what is new or settled no less often than readers of an ordinary blog, the discipline adds constraint without improving trust.

## What this group obliges

Read the date and evidence class on any item before you treat it as current — news decays like everything else. Follow a curriculum release to its changelog entry, and a research brief to its evidence class. And if you find an error in a published item, file it for the corrections log: the estate does not silently edit its news, which is the memory discipline the whole Library protects.
