# The estate's memory, kept so nothing is quietly changed

The Record is the group that holds the estate's memory — its governing instruments, its corrections, its verifications, and its errata. It exists because the estate's correctability DNA requires that a correction outsiders cannot see into is not a correction, and memory that can be quietly altered is not memory. This group keeps the MX instruments MX-000 through MX-022, the AG instruments, the corrections log, the verification log, and the errata, so a reader can see not just what the estate believes now but what it believed, what it got wrong, and why it changed. Its five pages are the MX instruments, the AG instruments, the corrections log, the verification log, and the errata.

## Why the record is kept in public and never deleted

The custody harm the License names is that a record readable only by the system that wrote it, and deletable at will, preserves nothing deliberately and forgets nothing a person needs forgotten. The Record answers it with a discipline: nothing is deleted, only superseded with a dated header, so an old citation still resolves to the version that was current when it was written. The corrections log publishes accepted and rejected corrections with reasons, so the estate's self-correction is inspectable rather than trusted. This is the estate's memory kept the way the License says a record should be kept.

## The map — what lives in this group

**MX Instruments (MX-000–MX-022)** are the estate's governing documents, each by immortal identifier. **AG Instruments (AG-000–AG-009)** are the agent-governance instruments. **Corrections Log** publishes every correction, accepted and rejected, with reasons. **Verification Log** records what has been verified and what carries an unchecked [VERIFY] tag. **Errata** lists known errors and their corrections.

## The worked instance — the corrections log

The corrections log is the group's clearest instance of the estate's correctability DNA. When a claim anywhere in the estate is found wrong, the correction is dated, its reason is stated, and — crucially — rejected corrections are published alongside accepted ones, with the reason for rejection. This is what distinguishes a real correction process from a private one: an outsider can see not only what the estate changed but what it declined to change and why. A correction queue outsiders cannot see into would be the exact failure the estate's DNA names, and the corrections log refuses it.

## Limits of this group

The Record documents the estate's memory; it does not itself decide what is true — that is the epistemics machinery. It keeps what was written, which means it also keeps superseded and mistaken versions, marked as such, rather than a clean revised history. And the verification log's [VERIFY] tags mark claims that are checkable but unchecked, which is an honest admission of incompleteness rather than a defect.

## The falsifier for this group

The group's governing claim is that a public, append-only record with published corrections builds more warranted trust than a quietly revised one. The refutation: if readers with access to the corrections log trust the estate no more, and catch its errors no better, than readers without it, the record adds transparency without benefit.

## What this group obliges

Cite instruments by their immortal identifier, so your citation survives revision. Read the corrections log to see the estate's self-correction in the open, including what it declined to change. And if you find an error, file it — it will enter the corrections log, accepted or rejected, with a reason, which is the estate holding itself to its own memory discipline.
