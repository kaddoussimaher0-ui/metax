## Why not everything at once?

TopTech alone is 2 flagships and 21 series. At the default cadence of **12 courses × 6 lessons** per series, that is well over a thousand individual lessons — each with a lab, an artifact, a pre-registered hypothesis, and a decay ledger entry. Dumping all of that in one release would guarantee one of two things: shallow, padded content, or a long silence followed by a wall of material nobody could realistically work through.

We would rather publish **less, sooner, and better** — then keep shipping on a predictable schedule you can plan your own learning around. A course that arrives late but survives scrutiny is worth more than ten that arrive on time and collapse the moment a platform changes its rules. That is the trade we are making, deliberately and in public.

There is a second reason, and it is about trust. When an academy dumps everything at once, it is telling you the material is finished — that it will not change. But the machine-mediated internet does not stop moving. Answer engines rewrite their retrieval logic. Ad platforms deprecate targeting. Social graphs reweight distribution overnight. A curriculum that pretends to be finished is a curriculum that is already decaying silently. Waves let us keep each series **current** rather than frozen.

## What "published" means here

A course is only marked live when it clears our quality gate. This is the single most important commitment in this post, because it is what separates a curriculum from a content dump:

- Every lesson runs the full arc: *What is · Why · How · Lab · Artifact · Scenario Assessment*. No lesson is a talking-head monologue.
- It ships a **90-day capstone** and a pre-registered hypothesis with an explicit null and a decision rule written *before* the results.
- Each strong claim is dated and classed **Durable, Semi-durable, or Perishable** in the decay ledger, so you always know how long a tactic is expected to hold.
- Every series embeds the six companion rails — compliance, accessibility, decay-awareness, falsifiability, kill criteria, and unit economics.
- Nothing is edited silently. Changes appear in public patch notes with a version number and a date.

> Completion is attendance. Competence is evidence. Mastery is judgment under scrutiny.

## How a wave actually ships

Behind each wave date is a fixed pipeline. First the outline is locked and reviewed by a second operator. Then lesson scripts are drafted, and every lab is built and run end-to-end by someone who did not write it — if the lab cannot be completed from the instructions alone, it goes back. Only then does the pre-registration get filed, the decay ledger get populated, and the accessibility and compliance passes run. A wave "ships" when every course in it has cleared all of those gates, not when a calendar square is reached.

This is slower. It is also the only way we can honestly tell you that what you are learning has been stress-tested rather than merely recorded.
