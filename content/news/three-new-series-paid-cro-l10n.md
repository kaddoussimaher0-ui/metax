Three new series join the TopTech stack, closing the last gaps in the operator's journey: **Paid Media & Retail Media Systems, CRO & Experimentation Craft, and Localization & Internationalization**. Together they take the next-level stack from eighteen to twenty-one series, and they are not filler — each answers a question operators kept running into that the earlier series pointed at but did not fully own.

## Series 19 — Paid Media & Retail Media Systems

Buying attention without being bought. This series is about spending money to acquire demand in a way that survives audit — auction mechanics, retail media networks, and disciplined spend across Meta, Google, TikTok and Amazon Ads. The organising principle is that paid media is a *system*, not a series of one-off campaigns, and that a system without kill criteria is just a faster way to lose money.

- **Auction literacy.** How each platform's auction actually clears, what you are really bidding on, and why "lowering your CPC" is often the wrong goal.
- **Retail media.** The fastest-growing ad surface of the decade, where the shelf and the ad are the same pixel.
- **Kill criteria and unit economics, baked in.** Every tactic is expressed as a cost-per-outcome, and every campaign carries the conditions under which you stop it. You never scale a losing bet because you were emotionally attached to it.

The discipline here is the same one that runs through the whole academy: a paid strategy you cannot express in unit economics is not a strategy, it is a hope with a budget attached.

## Series 20 — CRO & Experimentation Craft

Improving what already works. Most operators are far better at getting traffic than at converting it, and worse still at knowing whether a "win" was real. This series is about trustworthy online experiments, honest statistics, and the unglamorous difference between a genuine lift and noise dressed up as a result.

- **Trustworthy experiments.** How to run tests that do not lie to you: sample size before you start, one decision rule, no peeking-and-stopping.
- **Honest statistics.** What a p-value does and does not tell you, why most reported "23% uplifts" evaporate, and how to size an effect you would actually act on.
- **Learning faster than competitors.** The compounding advantage is not any single winning variant; it is a reliable *process* for turning uncertainty into knowledge.

This is how you learn faster than competitors instead of guessing louder — and it is the natural companion to the analytics and causal-measurement work earlier in the stack.

## Series 21 — Localization & Internationalization

Ship into every market. Growth eventually runs into borders — of language, currency, regulation, and culture — and most operators either ignore them or flatten them badly. This series covers i18n and l10n done properly, cross-border compliance, and the craft of adapting an offer to a place without erasing what made it work.

- **Internationalization (i18n).** Building so that new markets are a configuration change, not a rewrite.
- **Localization (l10n).** Translation is the easy 20%; the hard 80% is meaning, trust signals, payment norms, and local proof.
- **Cross-border compliance.** Data-residency, consent, and tax rules that will end a launch if you learn them after you ship rather than before.
