# The register the estate keeps against itself

The defect log is where the estate lists its own faults, by identifier, with a status and a resolution note for each. It exists because the estate names ethics laundering (HX-15) as a structural harm and defines the adverse-findings test — whether a party has ever published a finding against itself — as the cheap, hard-to-fake distinguisher between sincere and insincere adopters. This log is the estate's standing answer to that test: it publishes its faults continuously, so a reader does not have to trust that the estate is honest about its state and can instead read the faults and check.

# What a defect entry contains

Each defect carries an identifier (DEF-YYYY-nnn), a severity, a one-line description, a status — open, resolved, or deferred — and a resolution note stating either how it was closed or why it remains open. A defect is open until its resolution note says otherwise; naming it does not close it, and the log is careful never to imply that description is remediation. Defects are never deleted, only marked resolved with a dated note, so the log shows not just the current faults but the estate's history of finding and fixing them.

# The worked instance — the defect that cannot be closed by a machine

DEF-2026-056 is the log's clearest instance of the estate refusing to launder a gap. Part H's evidence must be checkable, which requires a full bibliography, and the register currently cites author-year without complete references. The obvious fix — have a machine assemble the reference list — is refused, because a machine-generated bibliography in a document whose whole claim is checkable evidence would be the synthetic-provenance harm HX-03 committed by the register against itself. So the defect stays open, names why, and waits for a human with the sources in front of them. That is the honest handling of a fault the estate could paper over but will not.

# Limits of this log

The log records the estate's own known faults; it cannot list the ones the estate has not yet noticed, which is precisely why the unnamed-harm review runs alongside it. Its severities are the estate's own judgement. And an open defect is open — the log does not imply that a fault appearing here is any closer to fixed than its resolution note states.

# The falsifier for this page

The log's governing claim is that publishing the estate's own faults builds more warranted trust than presenting only finished work. The refutation: if readers shown the defect log trust and use the estate less than readers shown only its polish, the log is a cost without a benefit — but the estate holds that a reader who can see the faults is better served than one who cannot.

# What this log obliges

Read it before you rely on the estate for anything load-bearing; it tells you what the estate already knows is wrong. If you find a fault the log does not carry, file it through the corrections log — the estate commits to publishing your report and its response, accepted or rejected, with a reason. And treat DEF-2026-061 as the estate holding itself to its own License: the ad slots are a named, open defect, not a hidden one.
