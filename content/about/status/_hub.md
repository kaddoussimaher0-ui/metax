# The layer where the estate publishes its own faults

State of the Build is the estate's honesty layer — the group that publishes, from dated data, exactly how much is built, what is broken, what is unanswered, and where the estate is going. It exists because the estate names ethics laundering as a structural harm, and an estate that named that harm while hiding its own incompleteness would be committing it. This group is the estate eating its own rule: the capacity meter states how much is real, the defect log lists faults by identifier, the determination queue shows open questions with timers that turn red in public, and the roadmap states gate conditions rather than dates.

## Why publishing the build state is a duty, not a courtesy

The register's HX-15 test is whether an adopter has ever published a finding against itself. This group is the estate's standing answer: it publishes findings against itself continuously. A visitor does not have to trust that the estate is honest about its state; they can read the defect log and the queue and check. This converts trust into inspection, which is the estate's core move applied to itself.

## The map — what lives in this group

**Capacity Meter** states, from dated data, how much of the estate is built, and keeps the eighty-percent capacity meter the spec requires. **Defect Log** lists the estate's own faults by identifier, including DEF-2026-041, the Pages file-ceiling arithmetic, and DEF-2026-043, the thin-by-nature page exemption, each with a status and resolution note. **Determination Queue** shows open questions with escalation timers that turn red in public when the estate is slow. **Migration Plan (VPS)** states how the estate would move off its current host if it outgrew the file or request budget. **Roadmap & Gate Conditions** states what unlocks each planned pillar, as conditions rather than promised dates.

## The worked instance — the determination queue's red timers

The queue is the group's sharpest instance of accountability. Each open question carries an escalation timer, and when the estate is slow to answer, the timer turns red — in public, where a reader can see it. This is the remedy-preservation harm answered structurally: rather than an internal backlog no outsider can see, the estate exposes its own responsiveness, and MX-016 ensures appeals are decided by someone other than the party appealed against. The red timer is the estate refusing to hide its own delay.

## Limits of this group

Naming a defect does not resolve it — an open defect is open until its resolution note says otherwise, and this group is careful not to imply otherwise. The capacity meter reports the estate's own estimate of its completeness. And the queue's timers depend on the estate actually filing its open questions; a question never entered cannot turn red, which is why the unnamed-harm review runs alongside.

## The falsifier for this group

The group's governing claim is that publishing the build state, defects, and open questions builds more warranted trust than presenting only finished work. The refutation: if readers shown the defect log and red timers trust and use the estate less than readers shown only its polish, the honesty layer is a cost without a benefit.

## What this group obliges

Read the defect log before you rely on the estate for anything load-bearing; it tells you what the estate already knows is wrong. If you find a fault the log does not carry, file it — the estate commits to publishing your report and its response. And read the roadmap's gate conditions rather than asking for dates: the estate states what must be true before a pillar goes live, not when it hopes to.
