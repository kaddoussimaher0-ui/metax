# The policy where the estate's money meets its own License

This is the page where the estate's funding model collides with the License it asks others to adopt, and it is written to state that collision plainly rather than to soften it. The content plane carries advertising slots. An advertising-funded surface engages the mechanisms the License names in HX-01 (attention expropriation) and HX-04 (the human as product). The estate does not pretend otherwise: its own Part H Statement marks both harms unremedied, dated, with these slots as the reason, and this policy is where that admission is explained in full.

# What the estate does and does not do with advertising

The estate carries advertising slots on its content plane, served by AdSense and Adsterra. It does **not** run behavioural profiling of learners, does **not** carry third-party analytics, does **not** sell or share learner data, does **not** optimise session duration, and does **not** place interstitials, countdown timers, or any of the dark patterns its commerce standard forbids. The advertising is a below-the-content revenue source on a demo, not a behavioural-targeting apparatus. That narrows the exposure — but it does not remove it, and the estate refuses to claim it does.

# Why the slots keep two absolutes unremedied

HX-01 attaches to systems that derive revenue from engagement, and an ad-funded surface does, even without duration optimisation, because impressions rise with attention. HX-04 attaches where a person is made the good transacted, and advertising implies a relationship in which the reader's attention is what is sold. Both are among the seven absolutes, which never scale down. Because the estate falls inside their bounded elements, the honest disposition is **unremedied** — not "mitigated," not "acceptable at this scale," but unremedied, with the remedy named.

# The worked instance — the remedy the estate has not yet taken

Since the site is a demo, there is a clean, cheap window to remove the advertising slots before the estate leaves demo. Removing them would put the estate outside the bounded elements of both HX-01 and HX-04, and the Part H Statement could then honestly read *remedied*. The estate has not yet taken that step, so the honest text stays: two unremedied absolutes, sitting two clicks from the license the estate asks people to adopt. This policy exists so that the choice is visible — keep the ad revenue and keep the honest marks, or remove the slots and earn the remedied disposition. There is no third option in which the estate keeps the revenue and claims the harms remedied.

# Limits of this policy

This policy describes the demo's current state and goes stale if the slots change; if they are removed and this page and the Part H Statement are not both updated, the estate has created a stale-disclosure defect against itself, filed as DEF-2026-061. The policy governs advertising only; behavioural experimentation and synthetic relation are addressed by their own harms, both inapplicable to the estate. And it is not legal advice — the estate's curator is not a lawyer, a fact the terms page states.

# The falsifier for this policy

The policy's governing claim is that disclosing the ad slots and their harm consequences in full serves a reader better than a boilerplate advertising notice. The refutation: if readers of this page are no better able to judge what the estate does to their attention than readers of a standard notice, the disclosure adds nothing — but the estate holds that a reader who can see the unremedied marks is better served than one who cannot.

# What this policy obliges

Read the estate's Part H Statement to see the two unremedied marks in their full context. Treat the ad-slot disclosure as the estate holding itself to its own register rather than as an apology. And if the slots are ever removed, hold the estate to updating both this page and the statement together — the estate has filed that obligation against itself as DEF-2026-061.
