# The page the demo strip on every page points to

This is the page the gold demo strip beneath every header links to, and it exists to answer one question honestly: on a site that is still under construction, how much should a reader trust any given page? The estate refuses the usual "under construction" banner, which tells a reader nothing, and replaces it with a three-way separation — what is real, what is scaffolding, and what is deliberately absent. Reading this page first is how a visitor calibrates everything else. It is the concrete form of the estate's own honesty: an academy that names ethics laundering as a structural harm cannot present a demo as a finished product.

# What is real

Some parts of the estate are load-bearing and can be trusted as they stand. The **design system** — the token layer, the component vocabulary, the chrome mechanics — is real and stable. The **routing law** is real: directory routes with a trailing slash, no `.html`, root-absolute paths, three-segment depth. The **navigation truth** in the routes data is real: every menu item, hub, and leaf resolves to a defined route. The **License and its register** are real: ML-2.3, Part H, the twenty-five structural harms across six strata, the seven absolutes, the roles, and the scale gate are written and governing. And the **estate's own Part H Statement** is real, including its uncomfortable marks: HX-01 and HX-04 stand unremedied because of the advertising slots.

# What is scaffolding

Other parts are placeholder-grade and must not be trusted as finished. **Prose marked scaffold** has not been reviewed and may contain errors the corrections log has not yet caught. **Counts and catalogue figures** render from dated data files that are themselves provisional — where a number appears, it carries its source, and the source is a demo dataset. The **planned pillars and groups** — Branch B and Branch C of TopTech, several Method instruments, the Meta-X programs beyond their charters — are scoped but not written, and their status badges say so. A page's stamp always states its evidence class and review date; a low class or an absent review is the page telling you it is scaffolding.

# What is deliberately absent

A third category is not missing by accident but excluded by principle. There is **no behavioural advertising and no third-party analytics** anywhere in the estate, which makes profiling of any learner structurally impossible rather than merely prohibited. There is **no companion surface** and none planned, which is why the synthetic-relation harm is inapplicable to the estate. There are **no uploaded images**, only shimmer placeholders and glyphs, which removes the surface through which most child-safety harm arrives. And there is **no live payment or entitlement** on the demo — the access pages describe an intended model, not an active one.

# The worked instance — how to read a single page

Take any leaf in the estate. Its stamp shows a status badge, an evidence class, a version, and a review date. If the badge reads `live` and the evidence class is E2 or higher with a recent review, the page is real and current. If the badge reads `planned` or `research-preview`, the page is scaffolding for something not yet built, and you should read it as an outline. If the prose is marked scaffold, it has not been reviewed. And if the page carries an `mx-thin` marker, it is thin-by-nature and exempt from the word floor under DEF-2026-043 — a schema or a cookie policy, not an essay withheld.

# Limits of this notice

This notice describes the estate's state as of its review date; it goes stale like any dated claim, and if it falls out of step with the actual build, that gap is itself a defect to file. It cannot make a scaffolding page real, only mark it as scaffolding. And it depends on the estate keeping its status badges and evidence classes honest — a mislabelled page defeats the notice, which is why the defect log runs alongside it.

# The falsifier for this notice

The notice's governing claim is that separating real from scaffolding from absent serves a reader better than a generic construction banner. The refutation: if readers shown this three-way separation misjudge which pages to trust no less often than readers shown only "under construction," the separation adds words without adding calibration.

# What this notice obliges

Read a page's stamp before you rely on it; the stamp tells you which category the page is in. Treat `planned` and `research-preview` pages as outlines, not conclusions. And when the estate leaves demo, expect this notice and the demo strip on every page to be removed together — until then, the strip is the estate telling you, on every page, to read this one first.
