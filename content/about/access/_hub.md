# The access model, stated on a demo that takes no live payments

Access & Money is the group that states how the estate intends to be paid for and gated — honestly, on a demo that currently takes no live payments and issues no live entitlements. It exists because an estate that hid its money model behind free access while planning to charge would be practising exactly the concealment its License names. This group states the intended pricing, what will always be free, how sign-in and identity work, what an entitlement is, and how refunds and disputes are handled, each marked clearly as intended-on-demo rather than live.

## Why the money model is published in full

Most sites reveal their pricing only at the point of payment, and their data practices never. The estate publishes both in advance, because a person cannot consent to an exchange whose terms are concealed — the *Gharar* the License's relational stratum names. This group is the estate pricing itself openly, including the uncomfortable disclosure that the demo carries advertising slots, which is why the estate's own Part H Statement marks HX-01 and HX-04 unremedied.

## The map — what lives in this group

**Pricing & Payment** states the intended model — Binance Pay only, stated plainly, no dark patterns — on a demo that takes no live payments. **What Is Free** names what will always be free regardless of the money model. **Sign-In & Identity** states that authentication is social-only, names the providers as text word-marks, and discloses that this currently excludes anyone unwilling or unable to use a listed provider. **Entitlements** defines what a paid entitlement grants and does not grant. **Refunds & Disputes** states how a disputed payment is handled, decided by someone other than the party disputed against.

## The worked instance — the identity exclusion, disclosed

The sign-in page is the group's clearest instance of the estate applying its own register to itself. Social-only authentication is convenient and secure, but it excludes anyone without an account at a listed provider, and it serves the Arabic-market audience worse than the English one. The estate does not hide this behind the convenience; it files it as both an HX-13 exclusion-by-default defect and an HX-25 linguistic-flattening defect in its own statement, and states it plainly on the identity page. The disclosure is the honest handling of a real exclusion the estate has not yet remedied.

## Limits of this group

Everything in this group describes an intended model on a demo, and each page says so; no page here transacts. The pricing rests on a payment rail the estate has not yet activated. And the identity exclusion is disclosed rather than remedied — the estate names it as a defect it owes rather than a problem it has solved.

## The falsifier for this group

The group's governing claim is that publishing the money and access model in advance, including its exclusions, serves a reader better than revealing it only at payment. The refutation: if readers shown the full model in advance are no better able to decide whether to engage than readers shown only a price at checkout, the disclosure adds nothing.

## What this group obliges

Read what is free before you weigh what is paid; the estate commits to a floor of free access regardless of its money model. Read the identity page's exclusion disclosure if you rely on non-social authentication. And treat the advertising disclosure as load-bearing: the ad slots described here are the reason the estate's own License statement carries two unremedied marks.
