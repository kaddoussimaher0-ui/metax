# The principles that constrain how the estate is built

Principles & Policy is the group that states the self-imposed constraints under which the estate operates — the rules it chose to bind itself with rather than the ones imposed on it. It exists because a set of principles that is not written down cannot be checked, and the estate's whole claim is that it can be checked. Its six pages state why the estate is static, how it uses AI and discloses that use, who governs it and in what roles, how accessible it is and where it falls short, how it handles security, and why it carries no uploaded images.

## Why these principles are load-bearing

Each principle in this group is a direct application of a structural-harm the License names. The static principle bounds the estate's own reversal cost, answering the successor-foreclosure harm. The AI-use policy discloses machine drafting on every page, answering the synthetic-provenance harm. The no-image regime removes the surface through which most child-safety harm arrives, answering developmental capture. The accessibility statement publishes its own conformance gaps as defects, answering exclusion by default. These are not generic policies; they are the register applied to the estate's own construction.

## The map — what lives in this group

**The Static Principle** states why the estate is plain files with forward-only migrations. **AI Use Policy** discloses how the estate uses AI and requires disclosure on every page under MX-007. **Governance & Roles** names who decides what, and the degraded-mode ceiling on what may be decided while the curator is unavailable. **Accessibility Statement** states WCAG conformance and publishes the gaps as defects. **Security Policy** states how vulnerabilities are handled. **No-Image Regime** explains why the design system carries no uploaded images, only shimmer placeholders and glyphs.

## The worked instance — the no-image regime

The no-image regime is the group's clearest instance of a principle earning its place. The estate renders `.img-slot` shimmer placeholders rather than uploaded images, and it does so for three converging reasons: images are the largest asset burden on a file-budgeted static host, they are the surface through which most child-safety harm arrives in comparable estates, and a design system that depends on bespoke imagery cannot be handed cleanly to a chartered academy. The regime turns an aesthetic constraint into a harm-reduction and a portability decision at once.

## Limits of this group

These are editorial commitments, not law; the estate enforces them on itself by discipline and public record, not by legal instrument. The accessibility statement publishes gaps, which means it admits the estate is not fully conformant — the honesty is the point, but the gaps are real. And the governance page names one curator, which the license's interpretive-concentration harm flags as a disclosed residual risk rather than a resolved structure.

## The falsifier for this group

The group's governing claim is that self-imposed, published principles produce a more trustworthy estate than unstated ones. The refutation: if the estate's conduct diverges from its published principles without the divergence appearing in the defect log, the principles are decorative and the group has failed.

## What this group obliges

Read the AI-use policy to know how the pages you are reading were drafted. Read the accessibility statement's gaps if you rely on assistive technology. And hold the estate to these principles: where its conduct diverges from what this group states, that divergence is a defect you are invited to file against it.
