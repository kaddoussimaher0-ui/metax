# The pillar that tells the truth about the estate itself

About is the pillar that turns the estate's gaze on itself. It states what the MetaX universe is, the DNA every pillar shares, who curates it, the policies that govern it, and — most importantly — the honest state of the build, because the live site is a demo and pretending otherwise would violate the estate's own License on the first page a visitor reads. About is where the estate's claims about honesty are made testable against the estate itself: a demo notice that names what is real and what is scaffolding, a capacity meter, a defect log, and a determination queue that turns red in public when the estate is slow. Its five groups are the Universe, Principles & Policy, State of the Build, Access & Money, and Legal & Contact.

## Why this pillar exists

An academy built on falsifiability and correctability owes itself the same treatment it demands of every technique it teaches. About is that self-treatment. It exists so that a visitor can find out, without asking, what the estate is currently lying about by omission — which counts are real, which prose is unreviewed scaffold, which pillars are planned rather than live, and where the estate falls short of its own License. The demo notice is the load-bearing page: until the estate declares itself out of demo, every page carries a provenance strip pointing here, and this pillar states plainly what that strip means.

## The map — what lives inside About

**The Universe** covers what MetaX is, its three pillars, the shared DNA, the curator Maher, and the demo notice. **Principles & Policy** states the static principle, the AI-use policy, governance and roles, the accessibility statement, the security policy, and the no-image regime. **State of the Build** is the honesty layer: the capacity meter, the defect log, the determination queue, the VPS migration plan, and the roadmap with its gate conditions. **Access & Money** covers pricing and payment, what is free, sign-in and identity, entitlements, and refunds and disputes. **Legal & Contact** carries contact, press, privacy, terms, the advertising policy, cookies and local storage, intellectual property, and the sitemap.

## How the estate holds itself accountable

The instruments in State of the Build are the concrete form of the estate's DNA. The defect log lists the estate's own known faults by identifier — including DEF-2026-041, the file-ceiling arithmetic, and DEF-2026-043, the thin-by-nature page exemption — each with a status and a resolution note. The determination queue shows open questions with escalation timers that turn red in public when the estate is slow to answer, so a reader can see the estate's responsiveness rather than take it on faith. The capacity meter states, from dated data, how much of the estate is built. None of these numbers is invented: each renders from a data file and carries its source, because a statistic without a date or a source attribute is exactly the dishonesty the estate's Method pillar bans.

## The worked frame — the demo notice

The demo notice is About's clearest instance of the estate's own standard. Rather than a generic "under construction" banner, it separates three categories explicitly: what is real (the design system, the routing law, the License and its register, the navigation truth), what is scaffolding (unreviewed prose marked scaffold, placeholder counts, the planned pillars), and what is deliberately absent (behavioural advertising, third-party analytics, any companion surface). A visitor leaves the demo notice knowing precisely how much weight to put on each page — which is the honest alternative to a site that hides its incompleteness behind polish.

## Limits of this pillar

About describes the estate; it cannot fix it. Naming a defect in the log does not resolve it, and the pillar is careful not to imply otherwise — an open defect is open until its resolution note says otherwise. The pricing and access pages describe an intended model on a demo that takes no live payments, and they say so. And the advertising policy must currently disclose the ad slots that keep the estate's own Part H Statement marking HX-01 and HX-04 unremedied — a disclosure the pillar makes rather than hides.

## The falsifier for the pillar

About's governing claim is that publishing the estate's own defects, capacity, and unremedied harms builds more warranted trust than a polished front page does. The refutation: if visitors shown the honest state of the build trust the estate less, and are less well served, than visitors shown only its finished face, then the honesty layer is a cost with no benefit and the estate is indulging a preference rather than serving a reader.

## What this pillar asks of you

Read the demo notice before you rely on any count or claim elsewhere in the estate; it tells you what to trust. If you are weighing whether to adopt the License or found an academy, read the estate's own Part H Statement and its defect log first — an estate that hid its own failures would have already answered the only question that matters. And treat the determination queue as an invitation: the estate publishes its open questions precisely so that a reader can hold it to the answers.
