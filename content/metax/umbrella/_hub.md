# The charter layer that governs MacroLifeTech's research

The Umbrella is the governing layer of MacroLifeTech (Meta-X) — the group that states what the research umbrella is, what it will and will not do, and how it holds its claims to evidence before any program reports a result. It exists because a civilizational research umbrella that began publishing before it had stated its charter and its exclusions would have no way to hold itself to a standard, and the estate's whole method is that the standard comes first. Its five pages are what MacroLifeTech is, the research charter, what this is not, the method-and-evidence policy, and the publication-and-preprint policy.

## Why the charter comes before the programs

The nine programs are speculative research lines, and speculation without a charter drifts into marketing. The Umbrella fixes the charter first: what counts as evidence in this pillar, how a preprint is handled, what the research refuses to claim, and what it excludes entirely. This is the same discipline the Method pillar applies to teaching, applied here to research — the apparatus that lets the estate hold a civilizational-scale claim to the same evidentiary standard it uses on a ranking algorithm. A reader arriving at the programs meets a chartered research umbrella, not a manifesto.

## The map — what lives in this group

**What MacroLifeTech Is** states the umbrella's purpose and its research-preview standing. **The Research Charter** fixes the governing commitments — the evidence floor, the human-subject and human-cell exclusions, the language-authority rule. **What This Is Not** states the ambitions the umbrella refuses. **Method & Evidence Policy** states how a claim is graded and cited in this pillar. **Publication & Preprint Policy** states how work moves from draft to preprint to publication under the publication-readiness protocol.

## The worked instance — the "what this is not" page

The "what this is not" page is the group's clearest instance of the estate's honesty. It states, before any program's ambitions are described, the things MacroLifeTech refuses to claim: it is not a claim that biology beats silicon today, not a promise that any program will succeed, not human-subject research, and not a marketing story dressed as science. Stating the refusals first is load-bearing in a research-preview pillar, because the refusals are where the umbrella draws the line a speculative program might otherwise cross.

## Limits of this group

The Umbrella charters; it reports no results — those would come from the programs, and the programs are research previews that have reported none. Its evidence floor is E1, the lowest, marking the whole pillar as a map of a frontier. And the Arabic text is authoritative for Meta-X under the language-authority rule, so these English pages are translations of equal standing but not the citable source.

## The falsifier for this group

The group's governing claim is that chartering a research umbrella before it publishes produces safer, not weaker, research. The refutation: if publishing the charter and exclusions in advance demonstrably constrains nothing harmful and only signals virtue, the charter is HX-15 conduct — compliance theatre — and must be replaced with enforceable structure.

## What this group obliges

Read the charter and the "what this is not" page before the programs; they tell you the standard the programs are held to and the lines they will not cross. Treat the E1 evidence floor as a statement of humility, not a defect. And carry the umbrella's exclusions forward under the License's successor stratum: a research-preview claim laundered into a certainty is exactly the harm the charter exists to prevent.
