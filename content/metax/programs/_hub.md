# The nine research lines the umbrella carries

The Nine Programs are the research lines of MacroLifeTech, each a program page carrying its own thesis under the umbrella's charter. It exists to give the umbrella concrete substance: an umbrella with no programs is a slogan, and the nine programs are where the civilizational questions the pillar asks are actually pursued. Every program is a research preview held to the umbrella's evidence policy, and one of them — BCIA — is deep and governed enough to have its own group. The nine are Agora (collective decision), Ma'nā (meaning and semantics), Metabolism (energy and flow), Soma (embodiment), Paideia (formation), Athar (trace and record), Āfāq (horizons), Mīzān (measure and balance), and BCIA (bio-computational).

## Why nine programs and one shared logic

The nine programs are not nine unrelated projects; they share a single logic proved first by BCIA — unify separated fields, swap a rigid assumption for a living one, and embed governance from the first line. Agora carries that logic into collective decision, Metabolism into energy, Soma into health, Paideia into learning, Athar into memory, Āfāq into space, and Mīzān into economy. The shared logic is what makes them one umbrella rather than nine, and it is why the umbrella's charter governs all of them at once.

## The map — what lives in this group

Each program is a leaf page. **Agora** studies collective decision. **Ma'nā** studies meaning and semantics. **Metabolism** studies energy and flow. **Soma** studies embodiment. **Paideia** studies formation. **Athar** studies trace and record. **Āfāq** studies horizons. **Mīzān** studies measure and balance. **BCIA** studies bio-computation and is deep enough to carry its own group and honesty layer.

## The worked instance — BCIA as the pattern

BCIA is the group's clearest instance of the shared logic, because it is where the pattern was proved and where the governance is deepest. It unifies information architecture with living substrates, swaps the rigid assumption that computation must run on silicon for the living one that it might run in cells, and embeds a full governance layer — the Cloister — from the first line rather than as a patch after disaster. That is the pattern the other eight programs inherit: not a technology, but a way of holding a civilizational question that builds the governance in before the research begins.

## Limits of this group

The programs report no results; they are research previews under an E1 evidence floor. Eight of the nine exist as chartered lines rather than as bodies of published work — their pages state their theses and their governance, not findings. Only BCIA is developed enough to carry its own group, and even BCIA is gated and pre-publication. The Arabic text is authoritative under the language rule.

## The falsifier for this group

The group's governing claim is that a shared logic — unify, swap a rigid assumption for a living one, govern from the first line — is a productive way to hold civilizational questions. The refutation: if programs built on this logic produce no better questions than programs built ad hoc, the shared logic is a branding exercise rather than a method.

## What this group obliges

Read the umbrella charter before the programs; it is the standard they are all held to. Treat each program as a research preview — a question the estate is pursuing, not an answer it has reached. And read BCIA's honesty layer, not its roadmap, as the real front door to the deepest program: it is where the estate shows a program holding itself to account.
