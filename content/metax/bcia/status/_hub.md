# The layer where BCIA argues against its own readiness

The Honesty Layer is BCIA's public status — the group where the program publishes, in the open, the reasons it is not yet ready. It exists because the estate names ethics laundering as a structural harm, and a research program that presented itself as finished while it was pre-publication would be committing it. This group inverts the usual research-program front page: rather than a brochure, a reader arriving here meets a critical audit that argues against BCIA's readiness, a remediation status, a publication-readiness protocol that must be satisfied before anything publishes, and a defect register of BCIA's own open problems. The demo strip is forced on every page here.

## Why a research program publishes its own audit

The estate's rule is that a correction outsiders cannot see into is not a correction, and the honesty layer applies it to a whole research program. Rather than let BCIA claim readiness and be checked later, the program publishes the audit against itself first, so a reader can see exactly where it falls short before deciding how much to trust it. This is the adverse-findings test the License names — whether a party has ever published a finding against itself — applied to a research program rather than to a company.

## The map — what lives in this group

**Status & Remediation** states where BCIA stands and what is being fixed. **The Critical Audit** is the document that argues against BCIA's own readiness. **Publication Readiness Protocol** states what must be satisfied before any volume publishes. **Defect Register** lists BCIA's own open problems by identifier. **Scope Limits & Open Problems** states what BCIA cannot yet decide.

## The worked instance — the critical audit

The critical audit is the group's defining instance. Rather than a defence of BCIA, it is an argument against it — a document that states, in the estate's own voice, the reasons BCIA is not ready to publish as fact. A reader arriving at BCIA does not meet a program confident in itself; they meet one that has written down, in public, the case against its own readiness. That inversion is what a research-preview program owes, and it is why the honesty layer is forced-demo and gated alongside the rest of BCIA.

## Limits of this group

The honesty layer states BCIA's status; it cannot make BCIA ready — publishing the audit does not resolve the problems the audit names. An open problem is open until the defect register says otherwise. And the whole group is gated and E1, because BCIA is pre-publication; the honesty layer is the most honest thing the estate can offer about a program that has not yet earned the right to report results.

## The falsifier for this group

The group's governing claim is that publishing a program's own critical audit before its results builds more warranted trust than publishing a roadmap. The refutation: if publishing the audit in advance changes nothing about how carefully BCIA proceeds and only signals virtue, the honesty layer is HX-15 conduct and must be replaced with enforceable structure.

## What this group obliges

Read the critical audit before anything else in BCIA; it is the program telling you where it falls short. Treat the publication-readiness protocol as binding — nothing in BCIA publishes as fact until it is satisfied. And read the defect register as BCIA holding itself to the estate's own standard: a program that publishes the case against its own readiness is one worth watching, precisely because it will not claim more than it can support.
