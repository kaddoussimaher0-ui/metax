# The deepest program, gated and governed from the first line

BCIA is the bio-computational program of MacroLifeTech — the deepest, most developed, and most governed of the nine programs, and the only one gated. It exists to study information architecture in living and embodied substrates with governance built in from the start, and it is gated because it assumes the Ascent's substrate literacy and carries an explicit human-cell exclusion a casual reader must not stumble past. The demo strip is forced on every BCIA page. Its ten pages cover what BCIA is and is not, the eight series, the volume roadmap, Document Zero and its seven bases, the charter and human-cell exclusion, the Cloister/Atrium governance, the notation, the bibliography, the contribution guidelines, and the locked gateway.

## Why BCIA is gated and governed from the first line

A program touching living substrates carries a moral weight beyond any ordinary research, so BCIA embeds its governance — the Cloister — before its first result rather than after its first incident. The gating is a real condition, not scarcity: BCIA assumes substrate literacy the Ascent teaches, and it carries a human-cell exclusion that must be acknowledged before entry. This is the successor-foreclosure and unconsented-experiment harms the License names, answered by exclusion and governance stated in advance — a red line drawn before any result exists to tempt the research across it.

## The worked instance — the human-cell exclusion

The human-cell exclusion is the group's sharpest instance of a red line stated in advance. BCIA commits, before any result exists, that it will not work with human cells — a red line printed where a reader cannot miss it, precisely because a red line that arrives after the temptation is no red line at all. The exclusion is the License's human-subject and unconsented-experiment harms answered by refusal rather than by protocol: BCIA does not need an ethics board to review human-cell work because it has committed never to do it.

## Limits of this group

BCIA is gated and pre-publication; it reports no findings, and its series and volumes are planned. Its evidence floor is E1. The most banned claim in the whole estate lives here — the DishBrain-style like-for-like efficiency comparison is a category error the banned-claims register forbids. And the Arabic text is authoritative under the language rule, so the English pages are translations, not the citable source.

## The falsifier for this group

BCIA's governing claim is that embedding governance and exclusions before research begins produces safer research than adding them after. The refutation: if the pre-stated Cloister and human-cell exclusion constrain nothing the research would not have avoided anyway, the governance is theatre and should be replaced with enforceable structure.
