# The three ways to climb the ladder, and the rule that binds them

Reading the Ladder is the group that tells a reader how to climb the Ascent — because the ten rungs are not meant to be read straight through by everyone. It exists because different readers climb for different reasons: some want substrate literacy for its own sake, some want only the bridge to biological computation, and some want the full ascent. This group offers three reading paths for those three purposes, points to the Arabic source books the pillar draws on, and states the citation-only rule that governs every rung. Its five pages are the blue, green, and gold paths, the Arabic source books, and the citation-only rule.

## Why the ladder has reading paths rather than one route

A single linear route would force every reader through material they do not need, which is the over-demand the estate warns against. The three paths solve this: the blue path climbs the foundational rungs and stops; the green path climbs as far as the bridge to biological computation; the gold path takes every rung to collective dynamics. Each path states its prerequisites and its stopping point, so a reader can choose the climb that matches their purpose rather than being made to summit when they wanted only the foothills.

## The map — what lives in this group

**Blue Path — Foundations** climbs the substrate and mathematical rungs for their own sake. **Green Path — Bridge to Life** climbs as far as the electron-to-cell crossing. **Gold Path — Full Ascent** takes all ten rungs. **Source Books (Arabic)** points to the ten-volume Arabic layer library the pillar reads from, under the estate's language-authority rule. **Citation-Only Rule** states the discipline that binds every rung — a rung may summarise and connect published work but may not assert an original empirical claim.

## The worked instance — the citation-only rule

The citation-only rule is the group's defining discipline and its clearest instance of the estate's method. It states that a rung page may summarise, connect, and interpret published work, but may not report a finding of its own, because the estate is not a laboratory and has no primary results. This is the synthetic-provenance harm answered in advance: by forbidding original empirical claims, the rule makes it impossible for the estate to launder a summary into a discovery. Where a rung touches a frontier the estate cannot responsibly summarise, the rule requires the boundary page to say so rather than paper over the gap.

## Limits of this group

The reading paths guide a climb; they do not shorten the material a path includes — the green path is shorter than the gold, but it is not a summary of it. The Arabic source books are authoritative under the language rule, which means the English rungs are translations of equal editorial standing but not the citable source. And the citation-only rule constrains the estate, not the reader — a reader may reason beyond it, but the estate's pages may not.

## The falsifier for this group

The group's governing claim is that offering three purpose-matched reading paths serves readers better than one linear route. The refutation: if readers who follow a path understand the rungs no better than readers who read straight through, the paths add navigation without comprehension.

## What this group obliges

Choose the path that matches your purpose rather than defaulting to the full ascent. Read the citation-only rule as the pillar's core discipline — it tells you the rungs are readings, not results. And under the language-authority rule, treat the Arabic source books as authoritative: the English rungs draw from them and must not be cited over them.
