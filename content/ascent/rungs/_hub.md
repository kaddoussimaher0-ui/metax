# The ten rungs from the substrate to the edge of silicon

The Ten Rungs are the spine of the Ascent — ten series, S38 through S47, that climb from the physical substrate of computation to the edge of what silicon can do and the beginnings of what might replace it. It exists to treat computation itself as the object of study rather than as a tool, which is what distinguishes the Ascent from TopTech. Every rung is a research preview: nothing here is taught as settled operator craft, each strong claim carries a citation, and the whole ladder is bound by a citation-only rule that forbids the estate from asserting original empirical claims. Each of the ten rungs is a leaf page carrying its own status.

## Why the rungs climb in this order

The order is causal, not arbitrary. S38 establishes substrate literacy — what a transistor, a gate, and an instruction set actually are. S39 examines the paradigms built on that substrate and their limits. S40 supplies the mathematical foundations. S41 marks the end of binary as an engineering choice rather than a law of nature. S42 explores multi-valued and fuzzy logics. S43 crosses from electron to cell. S44 examines biological computation, S45 embodied computation, S46 network science, and S47 collective dynamics. A reader cannot understand the biological rungs without the substrate rungs, which is why the ladder is climbed in order and why the gateway from TopTech gates on substrate literacy.

## The map — what lives in this group

Each rung is a leaf. **S38 Computing Substrate Literacy**, **S39 Paradigms & Their Limits**, **S40 Mathematical Foundations**, **S41 The End of Binary**, **S42 Multi-Valued & Fuzzy**, **S43 From Electron to Cell**, **S44 Biological Computation**, **S45 Embodied Computation**, **S46 Network Science**, and **S47 Collective Dynamics**. Each carries its own status in the rung-status table, and each is written under the citation-only rule.

## The worked instance — the end of binary

Rung S41 is the ladder's clearest instance of its method. Binary is not physics; it is an engineering choice that traded representational richness for noise immunity, and that trade is dated and specific. The rung reads the published work on multi-valued logic, analog and neuromorphic computation, and the thermodynamic limits of switching, and it states plainly what is established (binary's noise-immunity advantage is real and quantified), what is contested (whether multi-valued substrates can recover enough of that immunity to be practical), and what is speculative (that biological substrates dissolve the distinction). Three registers, three evidence classes, on one page — which is exactly what a research-preview rung owes its reader.

## Limits of this group

The rungs decide nothing empirically; they read. The citation-only rule forbids the estate from asserting an original finding, because the estate is not a laboratory and has no primary results. The evidence floor is E1, deliberately low, marking the ladder as a map of a frontier rather than a report from one. And several rungs are outlines whose prose is being written under the publication-readiness protocol, with their status shown in the rung-status table.

## The falsifier for this group

The rungs' governing claim is that treating computation's substrate as contingent produces better questions than treating faster silicon as inevitable. The refutation: if every rung's post-silicon alternatives prove to be dead ends and silicon scaling continues unbroken over the review interval, the ladder's framing is wrong and it should become a history of a settled question.

## What this group obliges

Read the citation-only rule before any rung, because it tells you exactly how much authority a rung claims — deliberately little. Climb in order, or take a reading path from the Reading group, because the biological rungs are unreadable without the substrate ones. And carry the ladder's humility forward under the License's successor stratum: a speculative rung laundered into a certainty is exactly the harm the estate's own gateway gates against.
