# The pillar that lets someone else build an academy on the same standard

Academies is the pillar that opens the estate's method to other people. Rather than keep MetaX a single curated academy, this pillar defines how a qualified curator founds and runs their own academy on the estate's infrastructure, held to the same rubric, the same rails, and the same public record. It is a governance pillar as much as an educational one: its subject is not a body of knowledge but a process — application, sandbox, provisional charter, chartered status, and finally institution — every stage of which is visible on a public record. Its five groups are How It Works, the Five Levels, the Eleven Requirements, Operating an Academy, and the Public Record.

## Why this pillar exists

An academy that cannot be reproduced is a personality, not an institution. If MetaX's method is real, it must survive being handed to someone who is not Maher, and Academies is the mechanism for that handoff. The pillar exists to make founding an academy honest in the two places it usually is not: cost and standard. It states the real cost of running an academy rather than the aspirational one, and it enforces a frozen rubric so that a credential from a chartered academy means what a MetaX credential means. The five-level ladder is deliberately slow — nobody arrives chartered — because the estate's credibility is shared across every academy that carries its charter, and one dishonest academy damages all of them.

## The map — what lives inside Academies

**How It Works** covers the four principles, the founding path, curator eligibility, the sandbox, the application, and the honest cost. **The Five Levels** are L1 Applicant, L2 Sandbox, L3 Provisional, L4 Chartered, and L5 Institution — a ladder with a status page each. **The Eleven Requirements** run R1 through R11, from curator eligibility through thesis scope, curriculum minimum, the frozen rubric, the governance charter, the technical spec, the bilingual policy, the legal terms, the red-line content bans, the annual audit, and finally R11, the mandatory Part H Statement that every academy must publish. **Operating an Academy** is the running manual: the technical guide and academy.json, the subdomain, the design constraints, moderation and enforcement, the academy exchange, and revocation and wind-down. **The Public Record** is the accountability surface: the directory of academies, the audit reports, the charter register, and the correction-bounty log.

## How founding an academy works

A curator applies (L1), is admitted to a sandbox where they build without issuing credentials (L2), earns a provisional charter that lets them issue marked credentials under supervision (L3), is chartered after an audit (L4), and only reaches institution status (L5) after a sustained record. Eleven requirements gate the path, and requirement eleven is the load-bearing one: every academy must publish its own Part H Statement, naming which of the twenty-five structural harms its deployment engages and how it handles them. An academy cannot be chartered while committing a harm it has not disclosed, which ties this pillar directly to the License.

## The worked frame — the frozen rubric requirement

Requirement four freezes the assessment rubric. A founding curator must adopt the D1–D5 rubric and may not quietly loosen it to pass more students, because a credential's value is the standard behind it. The worked instance: an academy teaching a practical craft submits its rubric during the sandbox stage; the audit at L4 checks a sample of issued credentials against that rubric; if the academy's pass rate diverges from its stated standard without a documented reason, the audit report says so in public and the charter is held. That is how a shared standard survives distribution.

## Limits of this pillar

Academies does not teach a subject; it governs the teaching of subjects by others. It cannot guarantee the quality of any single academy's content, only the honesty of its process and the integrity of its credentials. Some operating instruments — the academy exchange in particular — are live in specification but thin in practice while the estate is a demo. And the pillar cannot enforce its red lines by law; it enforces them by revocation and by the public record, which is a reputational instrument, not a legal one.

## The falsifier for the pillar

The pillar's governing claim is that a shared, audited standard lets many curators issue credentials that mean the same thing. The refutation: if credentials from chartered academies prove, on verification, to carry systematically weaker evidence than MetaX's own, then the charter is not transferring the standard and the pillar has failed at its one job.

## What this pillar asks of you

If you intend to found an academy, read the honest-cost page before the application; the estate would rather lose an applicant than have one discover the real cost after committing. Treat the eleven requirements as a floor, not a ceiling. And publish your Part H Statement early — under ML-2.3 the Statement is the compliance surface the whole License turns on, and an academy that files it late has already told its students something about how it handles disclosure.
