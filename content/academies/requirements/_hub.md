# The eleven gates that bind an academy to the standard and the License

The Eleven Requirements are the concrete gates an academy must satisfy to be chartered — the checklist that turns the estate's principles into things an auditor can check. It exists because principles alone cannot be audited: "be honest" is not a requirement, but "publish a Part H Statement" is. This group lists eleven requirements from R1 through R11, each a leaf page, and the eleventh is the load-bearing one: every academy must publish its own Part H Statement, which ties the whole Academies pillar directly to the License. Its eleven pages run from curator eligibility to the Part H Statement.

## Why eleven requirements and not a value statement

A value statement is satisfied by agreement; a requirement is satisfied by an artefact an auditor can inspect. The eleven requirements are all artefact-producing: R2 produces a stated thesis scope, R3 a curriculum minimum, R4 a frozen rubric, R5 a governance charter, R8 legal terms, R9 red-line content bans, R10 an annual audit, and R11 a published Part H Statement. Each is a document the annual audit can check against actual conduct. This is the estate's method — turn honesty into an inspectable procedure — applied to chartering.

## The map — what lives in this group

The eleven requirements are: **R1 Curator Eligibility**, **R2 Thesis Scope**, **R3 Curriculum Minimum**, **R4 Frozen Rubric**, **R5 Governance Charter**, **R6 Technical Spec**, **R7 Bilingual Policy**, **R8 Legal Terms**, **R9 Red-Line Content Bans**, **R10 Annual Audit**, and **R11 Part H Statement**. Each is a leaf page stating what the requirement demands and how it is checked.

## The worked instance — R11, the Part H Statement

R11 is the requirement that binds the Academies pillar to the License. Every academy must publish its own Part H Statement, naming which of the twenty-five structural harms its deployment engages and how it handles them. This is not optional and not a formality: an academy cannot be chartered while committing a harm it has not disclosed, and an academy that files its statement late has already told its students something about how it handles disclosure. R11 is where the estate's structural-harm register reaches every academy on the estate, not just MetaX itself.

## Limits of this group

The requirements are gates, not guarantees of quality — an academy can satisfy all eleven and still teach a weak subject, which is why the audit checks conduct against the stated standard rather than the standard itself. R9's red-line bans are editorial commitments enforced by revocation, not by law. And R7's bilingual policy applies the estate's language-authority rule, which some academies will find demanding.

## The falsifier for this group

The group's governing claim is that eleven artefact-producing requirements protect the shared standard better than a value statement. The refutation: if academies that satisfy all eleven issue credentials no more reliable than academies that satisfy fewer, the requirements add bureaucracy without protecting the standard.

## What this group obliges

If you found an academy, treat the eleven as a floor, not a ceiling, and produce every artefact they demand. File your R11 Part H Statement early — it is the requirement the whole License turns on, and lateness signals how you handle disclosure. And adopt R4's frozen rubric without loosening it, because the shared, un-drifting standard is what makes your credentials mean what the estate's do.
