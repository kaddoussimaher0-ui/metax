# The surface where every academy is held accountable in public

The Public Record is the accountability surface of the Academies pillar — the group where every chartered academy's conduct is visible to anyone. It exists because a charter that could not be checked would be worthless, and the estate's whole method is that claims are checkable. This group publishes the directory of academies, their audit reports, the charter register, and the correction-bounty log, so a prospective student, a peer academy, or a critic can verify what an academy claims against what the record shows. Its four pages are the directory, the audit reports, the charter register, and the correction-bounty log.

## Why the record is public

An audit no outsider can read is an audit that protects the audited, not the student. The public record inverts this: an academy's audit report is published, its charter status is in a register anyone can query, and the corrections filed against it are logged. This is the remedy-preservation and interpretive-transparency machinery the License names, applied to academies — a student relying on an academy's credential can see its audit history, and a correction filed against an academy cannot be quietly ignored because the correction-bounty log is public.

## The map — what lives in this group

**Directory of Academies** lists every chartered and provisional academy with its level and status. **Audit Reports** publishes the annual audit for each academy, including where its pass rate diverged from its stated rubric. **Charter Register** is the queryable record of charter status, grants, and revocations. **Correction Bounty Log** records corrections filed against academies and the estate's responses, with the bounties paid.

## The worked instance — the audit report that says an academy fell short

The audit reports are the group's sharpest instance of public accountability. When an academy's annual audit finds its pass rate diverged from its stated frozen rubric without a documented reason, the report says so — in public, in the record, where a student can read it before trusting the academy's credentials. The estate does not resolve this privately and publish a clean summary; it publishes the divergence and the charter action taken. That is what makes the charter mean something: it is backed by an audit a student can read, not by a badge an academy grants itself.

## Limits of this group

The record documents; it does not itself enforce — enforcement is revocation, which the record then reflects. On a demo, the directory and registers are lightly populated, because few academies exist yet, and the pages say so. And the record shows what the audits found, which is a sample of issued credentials, not a guarantee about every credential.

## The falsifier for this group

The group's governing claim is that a public record of directories, audits, charters, and corrections makes academy credentials more trustworthy than a private one. The refutation: if students relying on academies with public records fare no better than students relying on academies with private ones, the record adds transparency without protecting anyone.

## What this group obliges

Read an academy's audit report before you trust its credentials; it tells you whether the academy applies its own standard. If you find an error in any academy, file it for the correction bounty — the log is public and the estate responds. And if you run an academy, treat the public record as the point: your accountability is visible, which is exactly what makes your charter worth holding.
