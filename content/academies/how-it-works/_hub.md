# The honest overview of founding an academy

How It Works is the entrance to the Academies pillar — the group that explains, before anything else, what founding an academy on the estate actually involves. It exists because most "become a partner" programs hide the two things that matter most: the real cost and the real standard. This group states both. It lays out the four principles every academy inherits, the founding path from application to charter, who is eligible to be a curator, how the sandbox works, how to apply, and — the page the estate is proudest of — the honest cost. Its six pages are the four principles, the founding path, curator eligibility, the sandbox, apply, and the honest cost.

## Why the honest cost comes first

An applicant who discovers the real cost after committing has been misled, and the estate refuses that. The honest-cost page states, up front, what running an academy actually demands — in curator time, in review labour, in the discipline of freezing a rubric and publishing a Part H Statement. The estate would rather lose an applicant at the cost page than have one fail after founding. This is the same placement honesty TopTech applies to learners, applied here to prospective curators: match the person to the commitment before they make it.

## The map — what lives in this group

**The Four Principles** names the properties every academy inherits — decay-awareness, falsifiability, governance, and interlinking. **The Founding Path** walks the route from application through the five levels. **Curator Eligibility** states who can found an academy and why the bar exists. **The Sandbox** explains the supervised stage where a curator builds without issuing credentials. **Apply** is the application itself. **The Honest Cost** states what founding and running an academy really demands.

## The worked instance — the sandbox

The sandbox is the group's clearest instance of the estate's caution. Rather than let a new curator issue credentials immediately, the sandbox is a supervised stage where they build their curriculum, adopt the frozen rubric, and demonstrate the method — without issuing anything that carries the estate's charter. Only after the sandbox does a curator earn provisional status and the right to issue marked credentials under supervision. The sandbox exists because the estate's credibility is shared across every academy that carries its charter, and one dishonest academy issuing credentials would damage all of them.

## Limits of this group

This group explains the process; it does not guarantee admission — eligibility is a real bar, and not every applicant meets it. The honest-cost page states the estate's best estimate of the demands, which vary by academy. And while the founding path is live in specification, some later stages depend on instruments — the academy exchange in particular — that are thin in practice on a demo.

## The falsifier for this group

The group's governing claim is that stating the real cost and standard before founding produces better academies than a low-friction partner program. The refutation: if academies founded after reading the honest cost fail at the same rate as academies founded without it, the honesty adds friction without improving outcomes.

## What this group obliges

Read the honest cost before you apply; the estate would rather you decline informed than fail committed. Adopt the four principles as inheritance, not aspiration — they are chartering requirements. And treat the sandbox as protection, not delay: it is where you prove the method before your credentials carry the estate's weight.
