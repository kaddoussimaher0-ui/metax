# The manual for running a chartered academy day to day

Operating an Academy is the running manual — the group a chartered curator lives in once the founding is done. It exists because chartering an academy is the beginning, not the end: an academy must be run, and running it well means inheriting the estate's design system, moderating honestly, linking into the academy exchange, and — the page most partner programs omit — knowing exactly how the academy would be wound down or revoked. This group states all of it. Its six pages are the technical guide and academy.json, your subdomain, the design constraints, moderation and enforcement, the academy exchange, and revocation and wind-down.

## Why the manual includes its own ending

Most partner programs describe how to start and stay silent on how it ends, which is where the real accountability lives. This group states revocation and wind-down explicitly: the grounds on which a charter is revoked, and how an academy is wound down so its students are not stranded and its credentials remain verifiable. Stating the ending up front is the estate's honesty — an academy that knows the conditions of its own revocation runs differently from one that assumes permanence, and the estate's shared credibility depends on the difference.

## The map — what lives in this group

**Technical Guide & academy.json** states how to configure an academy technically, including the academy.json descriptor. **Your Subdomain** covers the academy's own subdomain, inheriting the estate's stylesheet unmodified. **Design Constraints** states what an academy may and may not change in the shared design system. **Moderation & Enforcement** states how an academy moderates under MX-008, including its child-safety provisions. **Academy Exchange** links chartered academies so mastery travels between them. **Revocation & Wind-Down** states the grounds and the process for ending an academy cleanly.

## The worked instance — inheriting the stylesheet unmodified

The subdomain and design-constraints pages carry the group's clearest instance of the estate's portability principle: an academy inherits `/css/style.css` unmodified. This is not an aesthetic preference — it is the no-image, portable-by-design regime applied to academies. An academy that could rewrite the design system would fork the estate's accessibility and performance guarantees; inheriting the stylesheet unmodified means every academy meets the same accessibility standard and carries the same bounded asset budget, which is what lets the estate charter many academies without each one becoming a separate maintenance burden.

## Limits of this group

The manual governs operation; it cannot guarantee an academy runs well, only state the constraints and the accountability. Some instruments here — the academy exchange in particular — are live in specification but thin in practice on a demo. And the design constraints are demanding: an academy wanting full visual freedom will find the shared stylesheet a real limit, which the estate accepts as the price of portability.

## The falsifier for this group

The group's governing claim is that a shared design system, honest moderation, and a stated wind-down produce more durable academies than a low-constraint program. The refutation: if academies run under these constraints prove no more accessible, portable, or accountable than academies run freely, the constraints add burden without benefit.

## What this group obliges

Inherit the stylesheet unmodified — it is what keeps your academy accessible and portable. Read the revocation and wind-down page before you launch, not after, so you run knowing the conditions of your own charter. And link into the academy exchange so the mastery your students earn travels, which is part of what the shared charter is for.
