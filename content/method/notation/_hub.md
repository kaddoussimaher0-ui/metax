# The grammar that keeps the estate's writing consistent and its research bounded

Notation & Research Ethics is the group that fixes two things at once: how the estate writes and cites, and what the estate's research will not do. It exists because an estate of hundreds of cross-linked pages needs a shared grammar — for symbols, identifiers, and versions — or its citations rot and its references collide, and because an estate touching research needs red lines stated in advance, before any result exists to tempt it across them. Its five pages define the symbol grammar, the identifier grammar, the versioning policy, the research red lines, and the human-subject exclusion.

## Why notation and ethics belong in one group

They belong together because both are about discipline stated in advance. The identifier grammar prevents a collision like the one the License's own drafting caught — where harms cited `H1..H14` collided with duty clauses cited `§H.1..§H.15` — by fixing zero-padded, immortal identifiers that are never renumbered. The research red lines prevent a different collision: between what a result might tempt the estate to claim and what it has committed never to claim. Both are the same move — bind yourself before the pressure arrives — applied once to citation and once to research.

## The map — what lives in this group

**Symbol Grammar** fixes the glyphs and symbols the estate uses and what each means. **Identifier Grammar** fixes the identifier scheme — MX-nnn, HX-nn, S##, DEF-YYYY-nnn — under the doctrine that numbers are immortal and never renumbered. **Versioning Policy** states how documents version and how a superseded version is retained. **Research Ethics & Red Lines** states what the estate's research will not do. **Human-Subject Exclusion** states the specific, absolute exclusion of human-subject research, which is why the Meta-X BCIA charter carries a human-cell exclusion.

## How the identifier grammar prevents rot

The grammar's rule is that an identifier, once assigned, is permanent and never reused or renumbered. When the harm register grew from fourteen to twenty-five, the harms were not renumbered; the citation form was changed from `H1` to `HX-01` so it would stop colliding with the `§H.1` duty clauses, and no number moved. This is why a citation written today still resolves years later: the target's identifier is immortal by policy. The versioning policy completes it — a superseded document is retained with a superseded header rather than deleted, so an old citation resolves to the version that was current when it was written.

## The worked instance — the human-subject exclusion

The human-subject exclusion is the group's sharpest instance of a red line stated in advance. The estate's research — most visibly Meta-X's BCIA program — commits, before any result exists, that it will not conduct human-subject research and, in BCIA's case, will not work with human cells. This is the License's unconsented-experiment harm answered by exclusion rather than by protocol: the estate does not need an ethics board to review human-subject work because it has committed never to do it. The exclusion is printed where a reader cannot miss it, precisely because a red line that arrives after the temptation is no red line at all.

## Limits of this group

The notation grammar governs the estate's own writing; it cannot compel an external citer to use it. The research red lines are editorial commitments, not law, enforced by the estate's own discipline and public record. And the human-subject exclusion is stated as an absolute of the estate's own research, which does not bind anyone else — it is a commitment, not a regulation.

## The falsifier for this group

The group's governing claim is that a fixed notation and pre-stated red lines produce a more durable and more trustworthy estate than ad-hoc citation and after-the-fact ethics. The refutation: if the estate's citations rot no less under the grammar, or its research drifts across its red lines despite them, the group's discipline is not doing its work.

## What this group obliges

Cite by immortal identifier, not by page position, so your citation survives revision. When you supersede a document, retain the old version with a header rather than deleting it. And read the human-subject exclusion as binding on the estate's research: it is stated in advance precisely so that no future result can be used to argue it away.
