# How the estate judges whether competence is real

Assessment is the group that defines how the estate decides a learner has actually learned. It exists because a lesson that produces no assessable evidence is a lesson that certifies nothing, and a credential resting on an unauditable judgement is a credential resting on trust. This group fixes the machinery that makes assessment checkable: a deterministic scenario engine with published scoring and no model in the loop, a frozen rubric that cannot be quietly loosened, a human viva for the judgements a scenario cannot reach, an adversarial review exchange, an artefact register, and an AI-disclosure standard. Several of its pages are planned rather than live, and their stamps say so.

## Why assessment must be deterministic where it can be

The estate's assessment principle is that a scored judgement a learner cannot reconstruct is a judgement they cannot contest — the due-process concern the License names in HX-06. So wherever assessment can be deterministic, it is: the scenario engine applies published scoring logic with no model in the loop, so a learner can see exactly why they passed or failed. Where judgement genuinely requires a human — the assessment of a novel artefact, say — it is handled by the mastery viva, with a named examiner, rather than smuggled into an opaque score. The line between the two is drawn deliberately, not by convenience.

## The map — what lives in this group

**Scenario Assessment (deterministic)** is the engine that scores a learner's response against a fixed scenario with published logic. **The Frozen Rubric (D1–D5)** is the five-dimension rubric every assessment uses, frozen so it cannot drift to inflate pass rates. **Mastery Viva** is the human examination for judgements a scenario cannot reach. **Adversarial Review Exchange** lets learners and reviewers test each other's work under stated rules. **Artefact Register** records the artefacts a learner has produced. **AI Disclosure Standard** requires a learner to disclose where a machine assisted their work, under MX-007.

## How an assessment stays contestable

An assessment produces a record a learner can inspect and appeal. The scenario engine's scoring logic is published, so a failing learner can see which criterion they missed. The frozen rubric means the standard did not shift between when they studied and when they submitted. And the appeals path — under MX-016, decided by someone other than the original assessor — means a disputed judgement is heard. This is the remedy-preservation duty the License names, applied to assessment.

## The worked instance — the frozen rubric

The frozen rubric is the group's load-bearing instance. A rubric that an academy can quietly loosen to pass more students destroys the meaning of the credential behind it. The estate freezes the D1–D5 rubric: a founding curator adopts it during the sandbox stage, and the annual audit checks a sample of issued credentials against it. If an academy's pass rate diverges from its stated standard without a documented reason, the audit says so in public and the charter is held. The frozen rubric is what lets a credential from a chartered academy mean what a MetaX credential means.

## Limits of this group

Several instruments here are planned rather than live — the scenario engine in particular — and their stamps mark them so; the estate will not present an unbuilt assessment as operational. Deterministic assessment deliberately excludes the judgements that require a human, which is a limit, not a defect: those go to the viva. And assessment certifies evidence of competence on a date, not present competence, which is why credentials decay.

## The falsifier for this group

The group's governing claim is that deterministic-where-possible, frozen-rubric assessment produces more contestable and more meaningful judgements than opaque or drifting ones. The refutation: if learners assessed this way perform no more reliably in the competence assessed than learners judged by conventional means, the machinery adds cost without signal.

## What this group obliges

If you build a course, end it with an assessable artefact and a deterministic scenario, not a quiz. If you assess, apply the frozen rubric without loosening it, and publish your scoring logic. And if you found an academy, adopt the frozen rubric as a chartering requirement — the shared standard is what makes every academy's credentials carry the same weight.
