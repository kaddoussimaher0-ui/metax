# The procedures that turn honesty into machinery

The Twelve Instruments are the estate's governance machinery — the concrete procedures that convert the estate's aspiration to honesty into steps a stranger can inspect and check. It exists because "we try to be honest" is not a method; a method is a set of instruments that operate whether or not anyone is watching, and that leave a record when they do. This group holds twelve such instruments, from the pre-registration desk that seals a hypothesis before data is gathered to the determination queue that publishes the estate's open questions with timers that turn red. Several instruments are planned rather than fully live, and their stamps say so.

## Why twelve instruments and not a policy

A policy states an intention; an instrument performs an action and leaves a trace. The distinction is the group's whole point. The pre-registration desk does not ask a researcher to be honest about their hypothesis — it seals the hypothesis, the null, and the stopping rule before the data exists, so honesty is enforced by sequence rather than by character. The decay watch does not ask anyone to remember to re-check a claim — it re-checks on a schedule. Each instrument removes a place where honesty would otherwise depend on someone choosing it, which is exactly where honesty usually fails.

## The map — what lives in this group

The twelve are, in order: **the Pre-Registration Desk**, which seals hypotheses before data; **Adversarial Review**, which tests a claim against its strongest opponent; **Decay Watch**, which re-checks claims on schedule; **Time-Boxed Cohorts**, which bound a course in time; **the Artefact Register**, which records what learners produced; **the Scenario Engine**, which scores deterministically; **the Mastery Viva**, the human examination; **the Academy Exchange**, which links chartered academies; **the Bridge Passport**, which gates entry across boundaries; **the Correction Bounty**, which pays for finding the estate's errors; **the Citation Ledger**, which tracks link rot and retraction; and **the Determination Queue**, which publishes open questions with escalation timers.

## How the instruments compose into governance

The instruments are not independent; they form a loop. A claim is pre-registered, adversarially reviewed, and placed under decay watch. If a reader finds it wrong, the correction bounty rewards them and the determination queue tracks the estate's response with a public timer. The citation ledger catches when a cited source rots or is retracted. And the artefact register and scenario engine handle the learner-facing side, feeding the credentials pillar. Together they make the estate's self-correction a machine rather than a promise.

## The worked instance — the correction bounty

The correction bounty is the group's clearest instance of enforced honesty. The estate pays a reader who finds an error the estate missed — which aligns the incentive of the outside world with the estate's own interest in being right. The bounty log is public, so the estate cannot quietly ignore a reported error: a reported defect enters the determination queue, its timer runs, and it turns red if the estate is slow. This is the remedy-preservation and adverse-findings machinery the License names, built as an instrument rather than pledged as a value.

## Limits of this group

Most instruments are planned rather than fully live while the estate is a demo, and their stamps mark them so — the estate will not present an unbuilt instrument as operational. An instrument leaves a record but cannot guarantee the estate acts on it; that is what the public timers are for. And the twelve are the estate's current set, not a closed list — the amendment procedure lets instruments be added or retired at review.

## The falsifier for this group

The group's governing claim is that honesty enforced by instrument outlasts honesty pledged as a value. The refutation: if the estate's self-correction proves no faster or more reliable with the instruments than a comparable estate's without them, the instruments are ceremony and should be dismantled rather than defended.

## What this group obliges

If you contribute a claim, pre-register it. If you find an error, file it for the correction bounty — the estate commits to publishing and responding. And read the determination queue as the estate's live scorecard: the red timers are the estate refusing to hide its own delay, which is the honesty this whole group exists to enforce.
