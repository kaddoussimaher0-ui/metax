# The six constraints that run through every lesson at once

The Six Rails are the constraints every lesson in the estate must satisfy simultaneously. Where the cognitive arc gives a lesson its shape in time — six beats in order — the rails run orthogonal to it: a single lesson must pass all six, at every beat. They exist because a lesson can be well-shaped and still wrong, still inaccessible, still teaching a tactic with no expiry and no exit. The rails are the estate's guarantee that a lesson is not merely well-formed but well-governed. Its six pages are the compliance rail, the accessibility rail, the decay-awareness rail, the falsifiability rail, the kill-criteria rail, and the unit-economics rail.

## Why six rails and not a checklist

A checklist is satisfied once; a rail runs the whole length of the track. The distinction matters because the failures the rails prevent are not one-time omissions but pervasive ones — a lesson does not become inaccessible in a single paragraph, it drifts inaccessible throughout, and a tactic does not expire at one point, it decays continuously. Framing them as rails rather than checklist items forces the constraint to hold across the entire lesson, which is why the estate can promise that any lesson, anywhere, carries an expiry, an exit, a cost, and a test.

## The map — what lives in this group

**The Compliance Rail** keeps a technique inside the platform terms and law it operates under. **The Accessibility Rail** requires the lesson to meet the estate's accessibility standard, with any gap published as a defect. **The Decay-Awareness Rail** attaches an expiry and a review interval to every claim, so nothing is taught as permanent. **The Falsifiability Rail** attaches to every substantive claim the observation that would refute it. **The Kill-Criteria Rail** states the condition under which a learner should abandon the technique entirely. **The Unit-Economics Rail** forces the true cost — in money, time, and attention — onto the page rather than leaving it implied.

## How the rails discipline a single lesson

Take a lesson teaching a growth tactic. The compliance rail checks it against the platform's current terms. The accessibility rail checks the lesson renders for assistive technology. The decay-awareness rail dates the tactic's expiry. The falsifiability rail states what observation would show the tactic no longer works. The kill-criteria rail states when to stop using it. And the unit-economics rail states what it costs to run and what it costs the person it reaches. A lesson missing any one rail is a build failure, because each rail prevents a distinct and common form of dishonesty.

## The worked instance — the unit-economics rail

The unit-economics rail is the group's sharpest instance, because it forces onto the page the number a vendor hides. A tactic that "works" is not a complete claim; the complete claim states what it costs to deploy and what it extracts from the person on the other side. The rail requires both. This is where the estate's method meets the License's structural-harm register directly: a technique taught without its attention cost is a technique taught without the HX-01 accounting the License demands, and the unit-economics rail is how the estate refuses to teach that way.

## Limits of this group

The rails constrain how a lesson is taught, not whether the underlying claim is true — that is the epistemics group's work. Some rails depend on external facts the estate cannot fully verify, such as a platform's current terms, so the compliance rail states its review date rather than claiming permanence. And the rails are demanding by design: a lesson that cannot pass all six is not published, which means the estate ships fewer lessons than a looser standard would.

## The falsifier for this group

The rails' governing claim is that a lesson passing all six carries less hidden dishonesty than one merely well-shaped. The refutation: if lessons that pass the six rails prove no more durable, accessible, or honest in practice than lessons that do not, the rails add constraint without benefit and should be reduced.

## What this group obliges

If you build a lesson anywhere in the estate, it must pass all six rails, and the decay-awareness and unit-economics rails in particular are where most lessons elsewhere fail. If you found an academy, the rails are a chartering requirement. And read the falsifiability and kill-criteria rails as the pair that keeps the estate honest: together they state what would prove a technique wrong and when to abandon it.
