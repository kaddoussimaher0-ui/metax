# The planned engineering track, marked planned rather than pretended live

Branch B is the engineering-and-IT track of TopTech, and it is planned rather than live — a status this group states plainly rather than disguising with polish. It exists to extend TopTech from operator craft into the technical disciplines that underpin it: design, development, data and AI, infrastructure, security, and networks. Because it is planned, every page here carries a planned badge and a low evidence class, and the group's honest posture is the point: the estate would rather mark a track as unbuilt than emit twelve-hundred-word pages for courses that do not yet exist, which would be the busywork harm the License names in miniature.

## Why a planned branch is published at all

An outline published as an outline serves a reader; a plan dressed as a product deceives one. Branch B is published so a learner can see where it fits, what it will require, and how it gates — the placement gate is real even while the courses behind it are scaffolding. This lets a learner plan a path through the estate that includes Branch B when it ships, without being misled into thinking it has shipped. The status badge does the honest work: it tells the reader, on every page, that this is a scoped intention, not a finished course.

## The map — what lives in this group

**Design & Interface (S22)** covers the interface layer. **Development (S23)** covers building software. **Data & AI (S24)** covers data systems and applied machine learning. **Infrastructure** covers the substrate a system runs on. **Security** covers defending it. **Networks** covers the connective layer. **The Placement Gate** governs entry to the branch — the real, live mechanism that decides when a learner is ready, even while the courses it gates are planned.

## The worked instance — the placement gate

The placement gate is Branch B's one live element, and it is the clearest instance of the group's honesty. Rather than let a learner into an engineering track they are not ready for, the gate states the prerequisite competence and checks it. It is published and operational now, because gating is real work that does not depend on the courses being written — the estate can honestly say who is ready for Branch B before it can honestly teach Branch B. The gate is thus the bridge between the planned courses and the live estate, and it is marked live where the courses are marked planned.

## Limits of this group

Branch B is planned; its course pages are outlines, and their stamps say so. Its evidence class is E1, the lowest, which is a statement of humility rather than a defect — the pages read as intentions, not conclusions. And the branch cannot certify anyone until its courses ship and the Credentials pillar can assess their artefacts.

## The falsifier for this group

The group's governing claim is that publishing a planned branch as explicitly planned serves a learner better than either hiding it or pretending it is live. The refutation: if learners shown the planned branch misjudge what is available no less often than learners shown nothing, the outline adds noise without value.

## What this group obliges

Read the planned badges as real: these are outlines, not courses, and treating them as live is a mistake the badges exist to prevent. Use the placement gate to know when you will be ready, even before the courses ship. And watch the roadmap's gate conditions rather than asking for a date — the estate states what must be true before Branch B goes live, not when it hopes to.
