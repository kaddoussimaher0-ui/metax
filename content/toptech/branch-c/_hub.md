# The planned track that teaches building an institution, not just operating in one

Branch C is the institution-building track of TopTech, and it is the bridge from operating on the machine-mediated internet to founding a durable institution on it — most directly, an academy under the Academies pillar. It is planned rather than live, and its pages are outlines marked as such. It exists because the estate's deepest claim — that its method can be handed to someone who is not Maher — requires a track that teaches the handing-over: how to build a curriculum, freeze a rubric, and govern an institution so it survives its founder. Its three pages are the academy builder, curriculum and assessment design, and governance and stewardship.

## Why institution-building is a distinct track

Operating a surface and building an institution are different skills. Branch A teaches the first; Branch C teaches the second, and the second is harder because it must produce something that outlasts the builder. This is why Branch C leans directly on the Method and Academies pillars: it teaches the frozen rubric because a credential's value rests on a standard that does not drift, and it teaches governance and stewardship because an institution whose meaning lives only in one person's judgement cannot be handed on — the interpretive-concentration harm the License names, met by teaching distributed, inspectable governance.

## The map — what lives in this group

**Academy Builder (S35)** teaches how to construct an academy on the estate's infrastructure — the technical spec, the academy.json, the subdomain. **Curriculum & Assessment Design (S36)** teaches how to build a curriculum on the six-beat arc and freeze a rubric that will not drift. **Governance & Stewardship (S37)** teaches how to govern an institution so it survives its founder, including succession and the degraded-mode ceiling on what may be decided in the founder's absence.

## The worked instance — freezing a rubric

Curriculum & Assessment Design's central lesson is the clearest instance of Branch C's purpose. A founding curator learns not just to write a rubric but to freeze it — to commit to a standard that cannot be quietly loosened to pass more students. The lesson teaches why: a credential's value is the standard behind it, and an academy that drifts its rubric to sell more credentials destroys the meaning of every credential the estate issues. Branch C teaches the freezing as a governance act, not a formatting one, which is why it sits alongside the Method pillar's frozen-rubric page.

## Limits of this group

Branch C is planned; its pages are outlines, and their stamps say so. Its evidence class is E1. It teaches institution-building but does not itself charter academies — that is the Academies pillar, into which Branch C is the bridge. And a learner cannot complete Branch C until it ships and its artefacts can be assessed.

## The falsifier for this group

The group's governing claim is that institution-building is a distinct, teachable skill whose absence is why most reproducible-method attempts fail. The refutation: if academies founded by Branch C graduates survive their founders no better than academies founded without the track, the branch teaches nothing the Academies pillar does not already require.

## What this group obliges

Read the planned badges as real outlines, not courses. If you intend to found an academy, read Branch C alongside the Academies pillar's founding path — the two are designed to be read together. And treat the frozen-rubric lesson as load-bearing: the shared, un-drifting standard is what lets a chartered academy's credentials mean what the estate's do.
